# The Villain Clock

## The Villain Clock of Echoes of the Fallen Dawn

This campaign uses a **Villain Clock** to keep the world moving without forcing the party down a single path. Threats advance whether the party intervenes or not—but *how* they advance depends on player choices.

The clock is not a countdown to failure. It is a **pressure system** that:

- Scales naturally with party level

- Rewards proactive investigation

- Keeps villains active without railroading

- Allows flexibility in travel and exploration

### Core Structure

There are **three interconnected clocks**:

1. **The Resurgence Clock** – Villains and threats returning to power

1. **The Legacy Clock** – The fate of the Fallen Dawn heroes

1. **The Revelation Clock** – The truth behind the real enemy

Each clock advances independently but can trigger effects in the others.

### 1. The Resurgence Clock (The World Clock)

**Tracks:** How much influence the old villains (or their legacies) have reclaimed

**Size:** 6 segments

This clock advances when:

- The party ignores a known threat

- A villain completes a major scheme

- Time passes without intervention

It slows or reverses when:

- A lieutenant is defeated

- A relic is secured or destroyed

- A region is stabilized

#### Resurgence Clock Stages

**1–2 Segments: Rumors & Omens**

- Strange creatures, minor cult activity

- Low-level encounters

- NPCs dismiss warnings

**3–4 Segments: Active Threats**

- Regions become dangerous

- Villain agents act openly

- Encounter difficulty scales to party level

**5 Segments: Regional Crisis**

- One location undergoes a major change

- Town evacuations, political pressure

- Boss-level lieutenants appear

**6 Segments: Villain Ascendancy**

- A major villain fully returns or transforms

- The world *expects* heroes to act

Note: This does NOT end the campaign. It changes the stakes.

### 2. The Legacy Clock (The Fallen Dawn)

**Tracks:** The condition and choices of the old heroes

**Size:** 5 segments

This clock advances when:

- A Fallen Dawn hero is found and confronted

- A hero’s past failure is uncovered

- The party chooses to involve or ignore a hero

#### Legacy Clock Stages

**1 Segment: Lost Legends**

- Heroes are missing, presumed retired or dead

**2 Segments: Broken Truths**

- The party learns *why* a hero vanished

- Failures, compromises, or guilt revealed

**3 Segments: Fractured Present**

- Heroes refuse to return—or act recklessly

- One hero begins acting independently

**4 Segments: The Turning Point**

- A hero makes a world-altering choice

- This may help… or harm

**5 Segments: Legacy Rewritten**

- The Fallen Dawn’s story is permanently changed

- One hero becomes something else

### 3. The Revelation Clock (The True Villain)

**Tracks:** The unveiling of the *real* antagonist

**Size:** 4 segments

This clock advances when:

- The party uncovers forbidden records

- Villains reference a shared unseen leader

- A Fallen Dawn hero’s actions stop aligning with reality

#### Revelation Clock Stages

**1 Segment: Inconsistencies**

- Contradictions in historical accounts

- Villains speak with reverence, not fear

**2 Segments: The Unthinkable**

- Evidence suggests a Fallen Dawn hero orchestrated events

- Archivist Elyndra grows hesitant, fearful

**3 Segments: The Mask Slips**

- The hero manipulates events openly

- Villains are revealed as tools, not masterminds

**4 Segments: The Final Dawn**

- The true villain is revealed: *A hero who decided the world must be saved from itself.*

### Using the Clock to Scale Difficulty

- Encounter difficulty scales with **current clock stage**, not just level

- Earlier intervention = fewer enemies, weaker magic, more allies

- Later intervention = stronger foes, altered terrain, political fallout

The party can go anywhere—but **no place is frozen in time**.

### Optional Twist: The Fallen Hero (Big Bad)

One member of the Fallen Dawn believed:

*“We didn’t save the world. We delayed it.”*

They now work toward a controlled apocalypse, believing only a remade world can survive.

- Early on, they appear as an ally or distant guide

- They help the party—*selectively*

- Their clock advances fastest when the party trusts them blindly

### DM Guidance

- Never announce clock ticks explicitly—show consequences

- Let players connect cause and effect

- When in doubt, advance **one segment, not all**

The villains do not wait.

But neither does hope.
