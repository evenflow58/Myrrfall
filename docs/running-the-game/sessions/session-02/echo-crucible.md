# Echo Crucible — Tabletop Run Guide

> **Myrrfall — Session 2**
>
> This document is the complete tabletop guide for running the Echo Crucible as a physical, player-mapped maze.
>
> **Important:** The maze below is the fixed layout. Do not redesign or redraw the underlying maze during play. The party discovers it gradually on the physical board.

---

# 1. Quick Reference

## Party

- 3 characters
- Level 2

## Encounter Goals

The party should:

1. Explore the Crucible.
2. Learn how its echoes behave.
3. Discover that echoes can guide them.
4. Learn that some walls are fractured memories rather than solid barriers.
5. Encounter the Echo Predator.
6. Discover that the maze itself can change.
7. Reach and solve the Memory Gate.
8. Stabilize the Core.
9. Leave the Crucible.

There is **no required order** for the major locations.

---

# 2. Physical Table Setup

Use the large player board.

The board starts blank.

The DM keeps the complete map in this document.

As the players explore:

- Draw walls on the player board.
- Leave corridors open.
- Reveal only the portion they have actually explored.
- Move the party's miniatures through the revealed corridors.
- Let the players make their own map if they want to.

Do **not** draw the entire maze at the beginning.

The maze is part of the encounter.

# 4. How to Run Exploration

There is no special movement system.

Run the maze as **exploration**, not tactical combat movement.

## Exploration Procedure

When the party reaches an unexplored area:

1. Ask which direction they want to explore.
2. Check the master map.
3. Reveal/draw enough of the corridor to reach the next meaningful decision point.
4. Describe what they see, hear, or feel.
5. Stop and let them decide again.

A useful amount to reveal at once is approximately:

- One corridor segment.
- One corner.
- One intersection.
- One dead end.
- One room.

Do not draw huge sections at once.

---

# 5. The Basic Player Experience

A typical sequence should sound like:

> **DM:** The passage ahead continues straight for about thirty feet before turning. You can also see a narrow opening to the left. What do you do?

Players:

> "We go left."

You check the master map.

If that section is a corridor, draw it.

If it is a dead end, draw the dead end.

If it reaches a marked encounter, stop and run that encounter.

Then repeat.

---

# 6. Dead Ends

Dead ends are intentional.

Do not avoid them.

The party should occasionally have to say:

> "Damn. We need to go back."

There are three kinds of dead ends.

## Ordinary Dead End

Nothing happens.

The party simply backtracks.

## Echo Dead End

The dead end contains a magical memory.

The party may discover:

- A ghostly spellcaster.
- Repeating footsteps.
- A spell repeatedly striking the wall.
- A figure attempting to walk through the wall.

## Secret Dead End

Something about the wall is wrong.

Possible clues:

- The wall has no reflection.
- Sound comes through it.
- The crystal pattern doesn't match.
- The wall flickers.
- A magical current passes through it.

Do not require a particular skill if the players themselves notice the clue and describe a reasonable way to investigate it.

# 7. Echo Casting

Whenever a spell is cast inside the Crucible, roll `1d6`.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | No additional effect |
| 4–5 | Minor Echo |
| 6 | Full Echo |

## Minor Echo

A harmless magical memory appears.

Examples:

- A previous Fire Bolt crosses the corridor.
- A ghostly Mage Hand repeats an action.
- A shield flashes into existence and disappears.
- A previous voice repeats the last word spoken.
- A spell's sound occurs several seconds after it was cast.

## Full Echo

A more substantial magical event occurs.

Examples:

- A ghostly spellcaster appears and repeats a spell.
- An old magical battle briefly overlays the current location.
- A previous spell affects the environment.
- A temporary Echo creature manifests.

**Important:** Echoes are not automatically hostile.

They are primarily a strange property of the Crucible.

---

# 8. Echo Density

Track the party's perceived Echo Density.

```text
ECHO DENSITY

1  □
2  □
3  □
4  □
5  □
```

Start at **1**.

Increase it naturally as the party gets deeper into the Crucible.

## Density 1 — Sparse

Occasional echoes.

The Crucible feels mostly abandoned.

## Density 2 — Noticeable

Ghostly figures and magical remnants appear regularly.

## Density 3 — Heavy

Multiple echoes can overlap.

The party begins seeing repeated events in different places.

## Density 4 — Saturated

The difference between present reality and memory becomes difficult to distinguish.

## Density 5 — Core

The party begins seeing echoes of themselves.

---

# 9. Echoes as Navigation

Early in the adventure, introduce a repeating ghostly figure.

Describe it:

> A translucent figure walks down the corridor.
>
> It takes exactly the same steps.
>
> Stops at the same place.
>
> Turns the same direction.
>
> Then disappears.
>
> A moment later, it begins again.

Do not immediately explain what it means.

If the players follow it, allow them to discover that it is traveling through the maze along a consistent route.

Eventually they should realize:

> **The echoes are not random.**

The deeper they go, the more frequently echoes appear.

After the Resonance Chamber, the party can intentionally use echo density as a navigation clue.

---

# 10. Location: Resonance Chamber

**Map marker:** `R`

## Purpose

Teach the party that the echoes reveal information about the Crucible.

## Arrival

Expand the corridor into a larger circular chamber.

Describe:

> The corridor opens into a circular chamber of translucent crystal.
>
> A crystalline structure stands in the center.
>
> Ghostly figures move through the walls around you, repeating actions that happened long ago.
>
> One figure enters the chamber.

## The Echo Sequence

The figure:

1. Walks to the first crystal point.
2. Touches it.
3. Walks to the second.
4. Touches it.
5. Walks to the third.
6. Stops.
7. Looks toward the party.
8. Disappears.

Then repeats.

## Solution

The party must reproduce the sequence.

Do not require a skill check if the players figure it out.

If they attempt the correct sequence:

> The crystals flare.
>
> For a moment, every wall in the chamber becomes transparent.

## Revelation

For several seconds, the party can see:

- Unexplored sections of the maze.
- Large concentrations of echoes.
- Several routes deeper into the Crucible.
- A powerful concentration of energy farther away.

Then the walls become opaque again.

Tell them:

> You realize that the echoes are moving toward something deeper in the Crucible.

### New Player Knowledge

The players can now use **echo density** to help navigate.

---

# 11. Location: Memory Well

**Map marker:** `W`

## Purpose

Teach the party about fractured/false walls.

## Appearance

> The corridor opens into a perfectly circular chamber.
>
> At its center is a pool of perfectly still crystalline water.
>
> The surface reflects the room exactly.
>
> Then the reflection moves differently from the room.

## The Memory

When a character looks into the pool:

They see an explorer from the past.

The explorer walks toward a crystal wall.

They do not open a door.

They simply walk through it.

The memory repeats.

## Discovery

The party learns:

> Some walls in the Crucible are memories rather than physical barriers.

From this point forward, allow them to discover certain secret passages.

Possible clues:

- No reflection.
- Incorrect crystal pattern.
- Sound passing through.
- A faint shimmer.
- A delayed reflection.

---

# 12. Secret Passage

**Map marker:** `?`

This is a hidden connection through a fractured wall.

The party does not automatically see it.

They can discover it after the Memory Well by investigating suspicious walls.

When they find it:

> The crystal wall ripples like the surface of water.
>
> Your hand passes through.
>
> There is a corridor on the other side.

Draw the secret passage onto the player board.

The shortcut should bypass part of the maze.

It is a reward, not a required route.

---

# 13. Location: Echo Predator

**Map marker:** `X`

## Purpose

This is the primary combat encounter in the maze.

The party should initially believe they have reached another dead end.

Then:

> You hear footsteps behind you.

Nothing is there.

Then:

> Footsteps ahead.

Three translucent figures emerge.

They resemble distorted versions of the party.

## Combat

Create simplified Echo versions of the characters.

They should use recognizable but imperfect versions of the characters' abilities.

Examples:

- Fighter Echo repeats a common attack.
- Spellcaster Echo uses a distorted familiar spell.
- Rogue Echo disappears and reappears.
- An Echo may repeat a maneuver the character used earlier in the maze.

Do not make the encounter excessively difficult.

The maze itself is the primary challenge.

## Reward

After defeating the Echo Predator:

> Something glitters inside the wall.
>
> Embedded in the crystal is a small crystalline fragment.
>
> It vibrates in response to the magic deeper in the Crucible.

The party gains the **Resonance Fragment**.

The fragment is useful in the deepest section and provides an advantage during the Core encounter.

---

# 14. Location: Echo Casting Event

**Map marker:** `1`

This is an early low-stakes magical event.

When the party reaches it:

> A burst of magical energy flashes across the corridor.
>
> For an instant, you see the ghostly outline of someone casting a spell.
>
> The spell repeats.
>
> Again.
>
> Again.

Allow the players to experiment.

If they interact creatively with the echo, reward them with information or advantage.

Do not make this a combat encounter.

---

# 15. Location: Dead-End Memory

**Map marker:** `2`

The party reaches a dead end.

Describe:

> The corridor ends in a smooth crystal wall.
>
> Then you hear footsteps.
>
> A translucent figure walks toward the wall.
>
> It reaches the wall.
>
> It walks directly through it.
>
> The figure disappears.

The figure repeats the action.

This is an important foreshadowing of the Memory Well.

If the players investigate the wall, they may suspect that it is not a normal physical barrier.

Do not require them to solve it here.

---

# 16. Location: Echo Trail

**Map marker:** `3`

This is a navigation clue.

When the party reaches the location:

> One translucent figure walks past you.
>
> Then another.
>
> Then another.
>
> Soon dozens of overlapping echoes are moving through the corridor.
>
> They are all traveling in roughly the same direction.

The party should begin to understand:

> The echoes are moving toward the deeper Crucible.

Increase perceived Echo Density by 1.

---

# 17. Location: Fractured Corridor

**Map marker:** `4`

This is the first strong reality distortion.

Describe:

> Your footsteps echo.
>
> Then you hear them again.
>
> Except the second set happens before you take the steps.
>
> Your reflections begin moving independently.
>
> One of them turns and looks directly at you.

Let the players interact with the effect.

Possible effects:

- A reflection walks down a different corridor.
- A character hears their own voice from ahead.
- A wall appears slightly displaced.
- A player sees the party from a different angle.

Do not make this a combat encounter.

The purpose is to establish that the Crucible is becoming unstable.

---

# 18. Maze Shift

Do not trigger this immediately.

The players should first learn how to navigate the maze.

A good trigger is:

- After the Echo Predator.
- After the party has explored a substantial portion of the maze.
- Or when the party reaches a major milestone.

## Description

> You hear a deep crystalline groan.
>
> The sound travels through the walls.
>
> Then silence.
>
> You look back.
>
> The corridor you just came through is still there.
>
> But it isn't the same corridor.
>
> One of the walls has moved.

### Physical Effect

Change **one connection** on the player board.

Do not rebuild the entire maze.

The party should feel:

> Our map is useful, but the Crucible can change.

## Later Shifts

Additional shifts can:

- Open a blocked passage.
- Close an existing passage.
- Create a shortcut.
- Remove a shortcut.
- Connect two previously separate sections.

Do not alter more than a small number of connections at a time.

---

# 19. Location: Memory Gate

**Map marker:** `G`

## Purpose

The Memory Gate is the culmination of what the party has learned.

By now they should understand:

- Echoes repeat.
- Echoes are memories.
- Magical patterns can be observed.
- Some patterns are stable.
- Some physical barriers are actually fractured memories.
- The Crucible's reality is unstable.

## Arrival

Expand the corridor into a larger chamber.

Describe:

> A massive crystalline gate fills the far side of the chamber.
>
> Four points of light hover around it.
>
> Each contains a distorted magical echo.

## Puzzle

Use the existing four-echo Memory Gate puzzle.

The party must identify and align the four distorted spell echoes.

If they have trouble, allow the earlier discoveries in the Crucible to provide clues.

### Success

> The four echoes suddenly align.
>
> The crystal gate hums.
>
> A line of light races across its surface.
>
> The gate opens.

---

# 20. Location: Core

**Map marker:** `C`

Run the existing Echo Crucible Core encounter.

The party must stabilize the unstable magical Core while dealing with its manifestations.

## Benefits from Exploration

The party can receive small advantages based on what they discovered.

### Resonance Chamber

They understand how the Crucible's energy behaves.

**Benefit:** One stabilization check can receive advantage.

### Memory Well

They understand fractured magical structures.

**Benefit:** Automatically identify one false/unstable element during the Core encounter.

### Echo Predator

They possess the Resonance Fragment.

**Benefit:** Use the fragment once to suppress one Echo manifestation or stabilize one point automatically.

### Echo Trail

They understand that echo density reflects the direction of deeper magical energy.

**Benefit:** Gain a clue about the next stabilization point.

These are optional advantages, not requirements.

---

# 21. Echoes of the Party

When Echo Density reaches 4 or 5, introduce echoes of the characters themselves.

Keep these harmless at first.

Examples:

- A character sees themselves walk around a corner.
- The party hears one character's voice before they speak.
- They see themselves standing somewhere they have not yet reached.
- An echo of the party takes a route they have not taken.

Eventually:

> A translucent version of one of you walks around the corner.

If a player asks:

> "Is that me?"

Answer:

> "Yes."

Pause.

> "But you haven't done that yet."

Do not explain it immediately.

---

# 22. Player Choices Matter

There is no required route.

The party may:

- Find `R` early.
- Find `X` before `W`.
- Miss `?`.
- Spend most of the session mapping.
- Get lost.
- Backtrack.
- Find shortcuts.
- Reach `G` without finding every encounter.
- Potentially reach the Core faster than expected.

Do not force them toward a specific location.

The maze should reward exploration rather than punish deviation.

---

# 23. Suggested Progression

This is the intended *experience*, not a required route.

## Phase 1 — Learn

- Enter at `S`.
- Introduce the first repeating echo.
- Explore corridors and dead ends.
- Find `1`, `2`, or other minor discoveries.

## Phase 2 — Understand

- Find `R`.
- Solve the Resonance Chamber.
- Learn that echoes can indicate deeper areas.
- Explore more aggressively.

## Phase 3 — Discover

- Find `W`.
- Learn about fractured walls.
- Discover `?` or another secret passage.

## Phase 4 — Threat

- Encounter `X`.
- Fight the Echo Predator.
- Gain the Resonance Fragment.

## Phase 5 — Instability

- Trigger the first Maze Shift.
- Encounter `4`.
- Begin seeing increasingly strange echoes.

## Phase 6 — Deep Crucible

- Echo Density reaches 4–5.
- The party begins seeing echoes of themselves.
- Find `G`.
- Solve the Memory Gate.

## Phase 7 — Core

- Enter `C`.
- Stabilize the Core.
- Use exploration discoveries as advantages.

## Phase 8 — Exit

- Leave through `E`.

# 26. Running the Maze Without Slowing the Session

Do not ask for a check every time the party walks down a hallway.

Use checks only when:

- They are investigating something.
- They are trying to identify a hidden passage.
- They interact with an echo.
- They attempt something risky.
- A specific encounter calls for one.

The maze itself should move quickly.

A good exploration rhythm is:

> **Describe → Draw → Choose → Reveal → Describe → Choose**

Keep it moving.

---

# 27. What Success Looks Like

The party does not need to "solve the maze."

Success means they leave understanding more about the Echo Crucible than when they entered.

Ideally, by the end they believe:

> The Crucible is not merely a ruined magical structure.

> It contains memories.

> Those memories form patterns.

> The patterns can influence the physical maze.

> The maze can react to them.

> And something deeper is using or shaping those patterns.

---

# 28. Tone

The Crucible should become progressively stranger.

### Beginning

**"This is a strange magical ruin."**

### Middle

**"This place is full of memories."**

### Late

**"Memory and reality are overlapping."**

### Near the Core

**"The Crucible remembers us."**

The party should leave feeling that they did not simply explore an ancient ruin.

**They were observed by it.**
