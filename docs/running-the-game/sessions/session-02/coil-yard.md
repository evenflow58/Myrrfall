# The Coil Yard

Heat rolls off the yard in waves of steam and ozone.

Crystal pylons lie in various states of disassembly, their fractured cores flickering with uneven blue light. Brass braces and iron cranes loom overhead, lifting jagged shards into place while workers shout measurements and scrape tools against glowing stone.

A half-repaired unit hums with a strained, off-key tone that vibrates in your chest.

Every surface bears scorch marks, chalked calculations, or hurried reinforcements.

This is not a place of invention.

It’s a place of constant repair.

**Visuals:**

- Steam cranes lifting cracked crystal pylons.

- Workers filing down jagged edges of refractive stone.

- A half-disassembled stabilization unit sparking intermittently.

**Possible Interactions:**

- Kular explaining how pylons “anchor wild surges.”

- A worker complains that surges are increasing faster than predicted.

- Thorn causes a nearby gauge to spike slightly when he walks past.

- Tordek notices dwarven engineering techniques in the pylon core.

- Kular sees Skylar studying a shard and warns him not to “touch anything glowing.”

**Lore Drop:** These pylons weren’t originally built here. They were adapted.

### Mira Flintgear — Senior Harmonics Engineer

**Race:** Rock Gnome **Energy:** Fast-talking, precise, permanently ink-smudged fingers **Role:** Stabilization specialist, Kular’s counterpart (and occasional rival)

Mira doesn’t believe the pylons are just degrading.

She believes they’re being *pushed.*

She speaks in rapid technical bursts:

“You don’t get harmonic drift like this from wear and tear. This is interference. Intentional or environmental.”

She respects competence immediately.

**Hooks:**

- Asks Skylar to stand near a resonance gauge (it spikes).

- Notices Thorn creates micro-feedback.

- Shows Tordek an original dwarven blueprint that’s been altered.

She suspects someone modified the original stabilization network.

She just doesn’t know who.

## The Calibration Contest

“Let’s see if the relief team can actually hold a harmonic.”

🎬 Scene Setup

Location

The Coil Yard.

Workers are gathered around a partially disassembled stabilization pylon mounted in a heavy brass frame. The crystal core inside it pulses unevenly, emitting an off-key hum that makes nearby tools vibrate.

Steam hisses from pressure vents. A nearby gauge cracks with a loud *snap*.

Mira Flintgear slams a wrench against the side of the frame.

“Again? No, no, no—that frequency drifted way too fast.”

One of the workers notices the party.

“Maybe the newcomers want a shot.”

🎯 The Goal

Stabilize the damaged pylon segment before:

- harmonic pressure overloads

- the frame ruptures

- the crystal destabilizes

This is NOT:

“roll until success”

This IS:

“manage a failing machine under pressure”

🧠 Core Structure

The pylon has:

THREE SYSTEMS

| System | What It Represents | What Happens If Ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harmonic Alignment | Magical frequency tuning | Wild surges |
| Structural Pressure | Physical containment | Explosive cracks |
| Energy Flow | Power routing | Arc discharges |

Each round:

- one system worsens

- players must decide who handles what

⚙️ TABLE SETUP

You can literally sketch this:

Each system has:

- Stable

Strained

🔄 ROUND FLOW

STEP 1 — Instability Roll

Roll 1d6:

| d6 | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Alignment worsens |
| 3–4 | Pressure worsens |
| 5–6 | Energy worsens |

That system shifts:

- Stable → Strained

- Strained → Critical

- Critical → OVERLOAD

❗ OVERLOAD

If any system overloads:

- contest immediately becomes chaotic

- everyone makes Dex DC 11

- 1d4 lightning/force damage

- contest becomes much harder

Not instant failure—but close.

🎮 STEP 2 — PLAYER ACTIONS

Each player chooses:

- which system to interact with

- HOW they help

This is where creativity matters.

⏳ WIN CONDITION

The party wins if:

All 3 systems reach STABLE simultaneously.

💥 ESCALATION

- crystals vibrating

- gauges cracking

- sparks increasing

Workers start backing away.

Mira starts yelling measurements.

The pressure should feel real.
