# Mira’s Price

## A Coil Yard bottle session for two level 2 PCs

**Expected length:** About 3 hours  
**Location:** The Coil Yard and Mira Flintgear’s harmonics lab, Refraction’s Reach  
**Absent PC:** Tordek  
**Central NPC:** Mira Flintgear, Senior Harmonics Engineer  
**Session goal:** Earn Mira’s analysis of the broken Shardfall pylon ring by helping her contain a live pylon accident and rescue her apprentice.

This is not a session in which the PCs simply arrive, ask questions, and receive a lore dump. Mira is present and talkative from the first few minutes, but she is trapped inside her lab with a live pylon, an injured apprentice, and a very good reason not to discuss Coil Yard evidence until the emergency is under control.

The PCs get the conversation in stages:

1. They negotiate with Mira through a window and speaking horn.
2. They prove they are competent enough to help.
3. They enter the lab and finish the rescue under pressure.
4. Mira examines the ring with them and tells them what it proves, what it suggests, and what it does not prove.

---

## Session at a Glance

### The situation

Earlier that day, Mira received a returned focusing ring from a maintenance crate. Its serial marks matched the same calibration shipment as the broken ring recovered at Shardfall. When she mounted the returned ring on her laboratory test pylon and brought it up to power, it produced an unnatural, precise frequency — the same kind of artificial “wrong note” she has been hearing in the Yard’s instruments.

Mira cleared the lab. Her apprentice, Jory Fen, stayed to record readings and carry the master shipment ledger. When the test pylon crossed its resonance threshold:

- A containment field snapped around the pylon.
- The lab’s harmonic door lock engaged.
- A lead-glass blast screen toppled and pinned Jory beneath it.
- Mira was trapped between the pylon’s containment bubble and the north wall.
- Shardlings nesting in charged component crates began waking up.

The PCs arrive minutes later. Mira can see and speak to them, but she cannot leave, Jory cannot free himself, and the pylon is still building charge.

### Why Mira won’t simply answer their questions

Mira has three reasons:

1. **People come first.** Jory is trapped in a room with a live pylon.
2. **The Yard comes second.** If the pylon vents, it could injure workers and destroy evidence all over the Coil Yard.
3. **She does not yet trust the PCs’ motives.** They have arrived carrying a Coil Yard-stamped component connected to a sabotage investigation. Until they demonstrate otherwise, she has to consider that they may be trying to pin the crime on her people.

Her position is not “do me an unrelated favor.” It is:

> “You want me to examine evidence that could ruin this Yard. Fine. Help me keep it from exploding, get my apprentice out, and then I’ll examine it properly.”

### What the PCs should learn

By the end of the session, the PCs should know:

- The Coil Yard stamp on the Shardfall ring is genuine.
- The ring belonged to a calibration shipment authorized under the name **V. Dorne**.
- The ring passed Coil Yard inspection before it was altered.
- The alteration happened after inspection, while the shipment was in transit or storage.
- The fractures in the ring are deliberate tool scoring, not storm damage or ordinary wear.
- The ring’s gain threshold was raised, causing the Shardfall pylon to broadcast a harmonic pulse and pull the storm toward itself.
- Mira has now heard the same artificial frequency in another ring from the same shipment.
- A second calibration set from that shipment is unaccounted for and was logged against the eastern maintenance route.
- The evidence creates a paper trail toward Velrix Dorne, but does **not** prove that Velrix personally altered the ring or reveal why he would do so.

### What this session must not reveal

Do **not** reveal:

- Velrix’s motive.
- Anything about resonance catalysts being used to stress-test the pylon network.
- The fate of Rhoric’s team.
- Anything about the Thirteenth Hour or the Sixth Fallen Dawn.
- A connection between Iri Vale and anyone else who shares the Vale name.
- Whether every pylon in the network has been altered. Mira has evidence of a pattern, not proof of its full extent.

### Pylon scale

The pylons in this session are human-scale machines, approximately **10 feet tall**.

Mira’s laboratory pylon is a crystal column mounted on a rotating brass base. Its three focusing rings sit at roughly chest, head, and just-above-head height. A technician can service it from the floor or a short step platform. It is dangerous because of the energy it channels, not because it is a monumental tower.

At standard 28 mm tabletop scale, a 10-foot pylon is approximately 2 inches tall.

## Preparation

### You need

- A simple tracker for the Calibration Contest: three systems, each marked Stable, Strained, Critical, or Overload.
- A rough sketch or battlemat of Mira’s lab.
- Four shardling miniatures or tokens.
- Tokens or notes for:
  - Mira’s one “Flintgear correction”
  - The Yard crew’s one “brace”
  - Chase successes and failures, if you run the optional chase
- The information checklist from the end of this document.

### Tordek’s absence

Handle it in one sentence and move on. For example:

> Tordek has remained behind to watch the party’s other evidence and recover from the night beneath the clock tower. The two of you have brought the ring evidence to the Coil Yard yourselves.

If your table has established that Tordek is physically holding the original ring, the PCs can instead bring:

- A detached section of the broken focusing ring
- A full-size charcoal rubbing of the stamp and scoring
- Measurements taken at Shardfall

Mira can work from any of those. The session does not change.

### New minor NPCs

Two names in this write-out are new session NPCs. Rename them freely if you already have people in mind.

- **Jory Fen:** Mira’s apprentice. Young, gangly, brave, and inclined to joke when frightened.
- **Dennet “Den” Rusk:** Coil Yard shift foreman. Broad, soot-streaked, protective of his crew, and terrified that the Yard will be blamed for Shardfall.

## Dramatis Personae

## Mira Flintgear

**Race:** Rock gnome  
**Role:** Senior Harmonics Engineer  
**Appearance:** Permanently ink-smudged fingers, soot-flecked goggles, tools arranged with obsessive precision even during an emergency.  
**Energy:** Fast-talking, exact, funny when she is frightened and does not want anyone to notice.

Mira respects competence immediately and has no patience for vague reassurance. She gives short instructions and expects people to act while she is still explaining why.

### Mira’s manner

She speaks in rapid technical bursts:

> “Don’t touch the red wheel unless you want to become part of the grounding system.”

> “That is not a hum. A hum has manners. That is a scream learning pitch.”

> “Good. Competent. I retract two of the things I was thinking about you.”

### What Mira wants

- Jory out safely.
- Her lab and workers intact.
- To know whether her Yard was used to facilitate sabotage.
- To avoid a public accusation until she knows exactly what the evidence proves.

### What Mira fears

- That someone inside the Coil Yard helped alter the shipment.
- That panic will shut down pylon maintenance across Refraction’s Reach.
- That the party will take a partial truth to Velrix and give him time to destroy the rest of the evidence.

### Mira’s attitude during the session

1. **Suspicious:** “You brought my stamp into my Yard and called it evidence.”
2. **Testing:** “Show me you can follow a technical instruction without improvising your own funeral.”
3. **Trusting:** “You got Jory out. Now put the ring on the plate. Let’s find out who used my Yard.”
4. **Furious on their behalf:** “Somebody used my inspection seal as a blindfold. I do not care for that.”

## Jory Fen

**Role:** Mira’s apprentice  
**Appearance:** Young human, long limbs, one boot trapped beneath the fallen blast screen.  
**Energy:** Scared, in pain, and determined to be useful.

Jory carried the master shipment ledger into the lab because Mira wanted the record beside her while she tested the returned ring. The ledger case is now trapped near him beneath the edge of the blast screen.

Jory knows:

- Mira cleared the lab because the returned ring “sang wrong.”
- She told him to leave twice. He stayed because he thought she might need a second pair of hands.
- The containment sequence begins with Alignment and ends with Pressure.
- A salvage runner has been hanging around the Yard gate since the previous shift.

Jory does not know who altered the ring.

### Jory’s voice

He jokes when he is scared:

> “I’m fine. This is a load-bearing screen. I’m providing structural support.”

> “If Mira asks, I absolutely left when she told me to. Twice.”

> “Please don’t make the pylon angry. It has already expressed itself.”

## Dennet “Den” Rusk

**Role:** Coil Yard shift foreman  
**Appearance:** Broad-shouldered, forearms covered in old burns, carries a brass signal whistle he keeps forgetting to use.  
**Energy:** Loud, practical, and increasingly frightened underneath the bluster.

Den initially blocks the PCs because the lab is sealed and the Yard is under emergency procedure. He is not corrupt and is not protecting the saboteur. He is protecting his crew and trying to keep an accident from becoming a scandal before he understands it.

Den knows:

- Mira argued with a courier that morning about a ring that was resonating at the wrong frequency.
- She ordered the harmonics lab cleared.
- Jory followed her in with the shipment ledger.
- Mira told him, “If this is what I think it is, nobody touches the paperwork until I’m done.”

That last detail becomes important if the ledger page goes missing.

## The Runner

The runner is an unnamed salvage courier hired through a cutout. He has been waiting near the Yard gate for a chance to remove the counterfoil from the master shipment ledger.

He does not know Velrix’s plan. He was told:

> “Bring back anything with V.D. on it. Don’t read it. Don’t keep it. Don’t get caught.”

He is not willing to die for the job. If cornered, he drops the packet and runs. If captured, he bargains for release rather than fighting.

Use the chase only if the session is on schedule. The core information is never dependent on catching him.

### If you fall behind

Cut in this order:

1. Skip the runner chase.
2. Reduce the shardlings from four to three.
3. Shorten the Calibration Contest from six rounds to four.
4. Have Jory free himself from the second required rescue success once the PCs have lifted the screen enough for him to crawl.

### If you run ahead

Add in this order:

1. Give Den and the crane crew a short scene in which the workers debate whether to vent the lab immediately.
2. Let Mira answer two or three yes-or-no questions through the window during the investigation.
3. Add the runner chase.
4. After the ring examination, let the PCs decide whether to warn the eastern maintenance team immediately or keep the evidence quiet until Tordek returns.

# Scene 1: The Sealed Lab

**Target time:** 12 minutes

## Read aloud

> Heat rolls across the Coil Yard in waves of steam and ozone. Workers are moving quickly, but nobody is shouting. That is somehow worse.
>
> A brass alarm bell hangs silent above the yard. Beneath it, every worker keeps glancing toward a squat reinforced building marked with black-and-yellow harmonic warnings.
>
> Its crystal window is fogged from the inside. Its door is shut. Red warning crystals pulse above the frame.
>
> Through the glass, a shape moves. A small hand wipes a clear circle in the condensation.
>
> Mira Flintgear’s face appears. She raises her goggles, looks at the two of you, and mouths one word:
>
> “Finally.”

Den steps between the PCs and the lab door. He does not draw a weapon. He plants himself like a man accustomed to stopping crane loads with hand signals and confidence.

> “No. Absolutely not. That lab is under harmonic seal, the pylon inside is live, and I have two people trapped behind that door. Unless you are here to help, you can be somewhere else very quickly.”

Let the PCs explain why they came. When they mention Mira, the ring, or the Coil Yard stamp, Mira raps sharply on the window.

There is a speaking horn beside the door — a brass cone connected through the wall. Her voice comes through flattened and loud.

> “Den. Let them stay where I can see them.”
>
> “Mira—”
>
> “I did not say open the door. I said let them stay.”

She looks at the evidence case.

> “That’s the Shardfall ring?”
>
> “Or enough of it. Before you begin, understand my position. My apprentice is pinned under a blast screen in a room with an unhappy pylon. My lab is sealed. My yard is one bad pulse from becoming a crater with excellent drainage.”
>
> “You want to ask me about a Coil Yard stamp. I want my people alive and my lab intact. Help me achieve my list, and I will give your list my complete attention.”

## If the PCs try to force the door

The door is a harmonic lock, not a normal barred door.

- A Strength check DC 15 can make it shudder, but not open.
- Anyone touching the frame when it shudders makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 lightning damage.
- Mira immediately shouts through the horn:

> “Do not pry at a containment lock while it is deciding whether to be a door or a weapon!”

Den points toward the external control bench.

> “The lock answers to the lab feed. You want in, you stabilize the feed. That’s what the bench is for.”

This should point the PCs toward Scene 2 without punishing curiosity too harshly.

## If the PCs immediately offer to help

Mira softens by exactly one degree.

> “Good. Competence may yet save us all paperwork.”
>
> “Den will show you the bench. Jory is inside with me. He is alive, conscious, and making jokes, which means his leg hurts more than he is admitting. The pylon is behind a containment bubble with me. Do not ask me who stamped what until those facts have changed.”

## Mira can answer three kinds of questions now

She will answer questions about:

- Whether she and Jory are alive.
- What needs to happen to open the lab.
- Immediate safety instructions.

She will not yet answer questions about:

- The stamp.
- V. Dorne.
- The shipment.
- Velrix.

If pressed, she says:

> “I am not refusing. I am sequencing. Dying would make me considerably less informative.”

# Scene 2: Reading the Yard

**Target time:** 23 minutes

The PCs need to understand three things before starting the Calibration Contest:

1. The lab door is controlled by the external bench.
2. The containment sequence inside begins with Alignment and ends with Pressure.
3. Den’s crew can help once, if Den trusts them.

Do not require every clue. Each clue gives them an advantage or makes a later choice easier.

## A. Mira’s workbench

Mira’s exterior workbench sits beside the control bench, shaded by a canvas awning. Everything on it is arranged with obsessive neatness except for the objects she left in a hurry.

The PCs find:

- A harmonic trace slate still drawing a jagged line.
- A cup of tea with a skin forming on top.
- Three tuning forks laid in a precise row.
- A fourth space, empty.
- A note in Mira’s small, furious handwriting:

> **Not wear — scored. Same wrong note. Do not let Den vent the lab.**

### Checks

**Investigation DC 12**

The trace slate shows the lab pylon’s frequency pattern. One artificial spike repeats at exact intervals. Natural storm interference is irregular; this is not.

**Reward:** Advantage on the first Harmonic Alignment check in the Calibration Contest.

**Arcana DC 12**

The lab pylon is not malfunctioning randomly. Its containment field is cycling because the mounted ring keeps trying to correct toward a false threshold.

**Reward:** The PC understands that stabilizing the external feed will open the door, but the pylon’s inner bubble will still need to be shut down from inside the lab.

**Perception DC 11**

Through the lab window, the PC notices Jory tapping the floor in a steady rhythm. He is awake and trying to attract attention.

**Reward:** The PCs can communicate with Jory through hand signals during the investigation.

## B. Den and the workers

Den is watching a pressure gauge whose needle is creeping toward a red band. He answers questions while working. Do not make him stand still for an exposition scene; let him tighten valves, wave off workers, and shout instructions while he talks.

### What Den tells them freely

- Mira received a returned ring that morning.
- She put it on the lab pylon at low power.
- She ordered the lab cleared almost immediately.
- Jory followed her with the master ledger.
- The door sealed less than a minute later.

### What Den reveals if treated respectfully

A successful Persuasion DC 11, or simply asking what Mira said before she went in, gets:

> “She argued with the courier first. Said the ring was singing in the wrong key. I told her returned stock sings all kinds of wrong after a storm. She told me storms don’t keep time.”
>
> “Then she took the ledger and said, ‘If this is what I think it is, nobody touches the paperwork until I’m done.’”

### What the crane operator adds

If the PCs speak to the workers rather than only Den:

- Mira cleared the building personally.
- She sent the senior technicians out but let Jory stay because he was recording readings.
- The pylon did not explode. It “caught” on a note and would not let go.
- A salvage runner has been loitering near the gate since the previous shift, asking when the Yard will reopen.

The runner detail is foreshadowing only. Nobody knows his purpose yet.

### Den’s trust

Den grants the crew brace if any one of these happens:

- A PC succeeds on Persuasion DC 11.
- A PC correctly identifies the danger from the trace slate.
- A PC asks about Jory before asking about the ring or the stamp.
- A PC volunteers to work the dangerous manual controls.

If none of those happen, Den still allows them to use the bench, but keeps his crew back. The PCs simply do not get the brace benefit.

## C. Through the window

Jory is visible when the condensation shifts. He is lying on his side beneath the edge of the blast screen. He can mouth words and use simple hand signals.

Through signals, Investigation DC 10, or a message spell, the PCs can learn:

- He points toward the pylon.
- He holds up one finger.
- He points to the glowing Alignment dial.
- He holds up three fingers.
- He points to the Pressure release.
- For the middle step, he points toward the conduit cabinet and mimes something flowing.

This establishes:

> **Alignment first. Energy second. Pressure last.**

If the PCs miss this, Mira can communicate the sequence later through the speaking horn, but it will cost them time during the rescue: she has to stop directing the Calibration Contest for one round to explain it.

## Fail-forward

If the PCs spend more than about fifteen minutes in this scene without moving toward the bench, the pressure gauge beside Den cracks with a sharp *snap*.

Mira’s voice comes through the horn:

> “That was the inexpensive warning. The next one will have opinions. Bench. Now.”

Den moves aside. Begin Scene 3.

# Scene 3: The Calibration Contest

**Target time:** 35 minutes  
**Purpose:** Stabilize the harmonics lab’s external power feed, unlock the door, and determine how dangerous the pylon remains when the PCs enter.

Use the three systems from the Coil Yard Calibration Contest:

- **Harmonic Alignment**
- **Structural Pressure**
- **Energy Flow**

All three begin at **Strained**.

## Read aloud

> The control bench is a slab of brass, crystal, and scarred wood built by someone who expected it to be used during emergencies.
>
> Three systems face you.
>
> A tuning dial sings faintly under its glass cover.
>
> A pressure wheel ticks, pauses, ticks again, each movement a fraction wider than the last.
>
> A row of crystal conduits brightens and dims in uneven sequence.
>
> Through the lab window, Mira presses one palm to the glass.
>
> “Six cycles before Den has to vent the room. I would prefer not to be vented. Begin.”

## Contest structure

The contest lasts a maximum of **six rounds**.

Each round:

1. Roll 1d6 for instability.
2. Each PC takes one action.
3. Mira comments, warns, or spends her correction if needed.
4. Describe the lab getting louder, brighter, or calmer.

### Instability roll

| 1d6 | System that worsens |
|---:|---|
| 1–2 | Harmonic Alignment |
| 3–4 | Structural Pressure |
| 5–6 | Energy Flow |

A worsening system moves:

- Stable → Strained
- Strained → Critical
- Critical → Overload

### Overload

If a system reaches Overload:

- Every PC at the bench makes a Dexterity save DC 11.
- On a failure, the PC takes 1d4 lightning or force damage.
- The overloaded system resets to Critical.
- Mira loses her correction for the rest of the contest if she has not already used it; she is busy keeping the pylon from venting.

Overload is dangerous and embarrassing, but it does not end the session.

## Player actions

A PC may work on one system per round.

### Harmonic Alignment

**Useful approaches:** Arcana, Performance, Insight, humming the pulse, tapping the crystal in rhythm, matching Mira’s hand movements through the window.

**DC:** 12

**Success:** Alignment improves by one step.

**Failure:** Feedback makes the next Alignment check at disadvantage.

### Structural Pressure

**Useful approaches:** Athletics, Smith’s Tools, Tinker’s Tools, bracing the frame, turning the pressure wheel against resistance, wedging a support into place.

**DC:** 11

**Success:** Pressure improves by one step.

**Failure:** A steam burst pushes the PC back 5 feet or deals 1 damage. Let the player choose which feels more appropriate to the action they described.

### Energy Flow

**Useful approaches:** Arcana, Sleight of Hand, Investigation, manually rerouting conduits, grounding excess charge, following the sequence of lights.

**DC:** 12

**Success:** Energy improves by one step.

**Failure:** An arc deals 1 lightning damage to the acting PC or the nearest ally.

## Working together

If both PCs work on the same system in the same round:

- If both succeed, that system improves by **two steps**.
- If one succeeds, it improves by **one step**.
- If both fail, apply only one failure consequence; they were sharing the same bad idea.

This makes coordination powerful without letting a single Help action solve every round.

## Mira’s Flintgear correction

Once during the contest, Mira can shout a correction through the horn at exactly the right moment.

The PCs may use it to:

- Reroll one failed check, or
- Learn which system will worsen at the start of the next round.

Mira’s correction sounds like this:

> “No — half a turn back. There. Do you hear it? Neither do I. That is the point. Hold it.”

## Den’s crew brace

If the PCs earned Den’s trust in Scene 2, his workers can brace one system once.

When a system would worsen, the PCs may spend the brace to prevent that worsening.

Den shouts:

> “Rusk crew! Hands on and pretend you like these people!”

The workers do not make checks. They simply hold the line for one round.

## Round-by-round escalation

Use these beats to keep the contest from becoming six identical rounds:

1. **Round 1:** Tools on nearby benches begin vibrating in time with the lab pylon.
2. **Round 2:** The fog inside the window brightens with each pulse.
3. **Round 3:** Jory stops joking. Mira glances toward him more often.
4. **Round 4:** A crack appears in the glass cover over the tuning dial.
5. **Round 5:** Workers begin backing away from the lab without being ordered.
6. **Round 6:** Den puts his hand on the manual vent control. He does not want to use it.

## Ending the contest

If all three systems are Stable at the end of any round after the PCs have acted, the contest ends early with a clean stabilization. Do not roll further instability once Mira confirms the feed is holding.

At the end of round 6, Den can safely force the manual release regardless of the exact system states. The PCs’ performance determines the condition of the lab when they enter.

### Clean stabilization: all three systems Stable

- The door opens with a soft chime.
- The pylon’s external feed is calm.
- The containment bubble remains, but it is weak.
- The pylon does not make lair-action attacks inside the lab.
- Award a **Stabilization Shard** after the session.
- Mira is visibly impressed.

Mira says:

> “All three. At once. Den, witness that. I may need someone to confirm I said something nice.”

### Partial stabilization: two systems Stable

- The door opens.
- The pylon remains live.
- Inside the lab, the pylon pulses at initiative count 20.
- Mira respects the result but remains brisk.

Mira says:

> “Good enough to enter. Not good enough to relax. Those are different categories.”

### Rough stabilization: zero or one system Stable

- Den forces the release during a venting pulse.
- Each PC makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 force damage as the door blasts open.
- The pylon remains live and unstable.
- Inside the lab, its pulse is stronger and the containment bubble damages anyone who fails to shut it down on the first attempt.
- The PCs gain a **Prismglass Fragment** rather than a Stabilization Shard or Damaged Pylon Component.

Mira says:

> “You opened it. I am choosing gratitude over precision.”

Regardless of result, proceed to the break and then Scene 4.

# Scene 4: Inside the Lab

**Target time:** 40 minutes  
**Purpose:** Rescue Jory, shut down the containment bubble, recover the master ledger, and deal with the shardlings.

## The room

Mira’s harmonics lab is approximately 30 feet by 25 feet.

### Layout

- **South wall:** The entrance door and the PCs’ point of entry.
- **Center:** The 10-foot test pylon on a rotating brass base.
- **North side of the pylon:** Mira, trapped inside the containment bubble.
- **East workbench:** Jory, pinned beneath the fallen blast screen. The master ledger case is trapped beneath the screen’s lower edge beside him.
- **West wall:** Conduit cabinets, grounding chains, and the Energy Flow valve.
- **South-east corner:** Open component crates where shardlings have nested.
- **Scattered throughout:** Workbenches, crystal samples, tool racks, and a shallow coolant channel around the pylon base.

The workbenches provide half cover if a PC explicitly positions themselves behind one. The coolant channel is difficult terrain for creatures that walk through it, except around the marked service plates.

## Read aloud when the door opens

> The lab smells of hot brass, ozone, and tea that has boiled dry.
>
> The test pylon stands in the center of the room — ten feet of crystal, brass, and bad temper, its three rings turning at slightly different speeds. Light moves up the column in pulses.
>
> Mira is on the far side, separated from you by a curved field of translucent energy. She looks furious, relieved, and determined to admit to neither.
>
> To your right, a lead-glass blast screen has fallen across the workbench. Beneath it, Jory Fen raises one hand.
>
> “Still providing structural support,” he says. “Any time you’re ready.”

Mira points in sequence:

1. At Jory.
2. At the three controls around the pylon base.
3. At herself.

> “Him first if you can manage it. Then the bubble. Alignment, Energy, Pressure. Do not improvise the order because you feel creatively oppressed.”

## Objectives

The PCs can pursue these in any order, though Mira will keep directing them back to Jory if he remains trapped.

### Objective 1: Free Jory

The blast screen is too heavy for Jory to move because its lower frame has caught on the workbench and one of his boots is trapped beneath a support strut.

Freeing him requires **two successful actions**. Useful approaches include:

- **Athletics DC 12:** Lift or lever the screen.
- **Tinker’s Tools DC 12:** Work the scaffold jack beneath the frame.
- **Sleight of Hand DC 11:** Release the boot clasp without shifting the screen.
- **Medicine DC 10:** After the first successful lift, guide Jory out without worsening the injury to his leg.
- A spell or tool that creates leverage, lubrication, a fulcrum, or a second set of hands.

Each failed attempt causes the screen to settle with a grinding shriek. On the second failed attempt, an energy arc flashes from the pylon: Jory takes 1d4 lightning damage but is not killed. Mira becomes much sharper after this.

Once freed, Jory can:

- Limp to the door under his own power.
- Tell the PCs exactly where the ledger case is.
- Once per encounter, throw a grounding chain at a shardling, imposing disadvantage on its next attack.
- If the PCs are struggling, crawl to the Energy valve on Mira’s instruction and hold it open, granting advantage on the Energy step of the bubble sequence.

Jory cannot fight effectively and should leave the room once the immediate area is safe.

### Objective 2: Recover the master ledger

The ledger case is a flat brass-and-leather box trapped under the lower edge of the blast screen, close to Jory’s trapped boot.

Once the screen has been lifted at least once, a PC can reach the case. Its clasp has fused from the heat.

Open it with:

- **Tinker’s Tools DC 11**
- **Sleight of Hand DC 12**
- **Strength DC 13**
- A careful application of cold, leverage, or a prying tool

If the PCs open it before freeing Jory, Mira objects:

> “Paperwork after people. I know the Yard has confused those priorities before, but let us attempt growth.”

Inside are shipment records, inspection marks, routing counterfoils, and Mira’s handwritten notes. The PCs do not have time to study it during the rescue. Mira needs it for Scene 6.

### Objective 3: Drop the containment bubble

Three controls are mounted around the pylon base:

1. **Alignment dial:** A brass dial set into the rotating base.
2. **Energy valve:** A crystal-handled valve connected to the west conduit cabinet.
3. **Pressure release:** A red-handled lever beside a squat vent housing.

They must be activated in this order:

> **Alignment → Energy → Pressure**

Each activation requires an action and a check:

- **Alignment:** Arcana or Performance DC 12
- **Energy:** Arcana, Sleight of Hand, or Tinker’s Tools DC 12
- **Pressure:** Athletics or Tinker’s Tools DC 11

A PC does not need to guess wildly. The controls brighten in sequence when approached, Mira can mime instructions, and Jory knows the order.

#### If the PCs activate a control out of order

- The bubble flashes.
- The activating PC makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 force damage.
- The sequence resets.
- The correct next control begins pulsing more brightly.

Do not reset the entire encounter or impose a long lockout. The point is pressure, not a guessing-game punishment.

#### If the Calibration Contest was rough

On the first failed sequence attempt, the bubble does not merely flash. It pushes everyone within 10 feet of the pylon back 5 feet. After that, it behaves as normal.

#### When the bubble drops

Read aloud:

> The third control settles into place.
>
> For one heartbeat, every light in the lab goes out.
>
> Then the containment field collapses inward with a sound like a struck wineglass, and Mira Flintgear steps through the fading rings of light with the expression of a woman preparing several complaints in alphabetical order.

Mira is free. She can now help directly, but she should not solve the remaining encounter by herself. She has no weapon ready, her hands are full of diagnostic tools, and her first concern is the pylon and Jory.

## The shardlings

### When they appear

The shardlings emerge when any of the following happens:

- A PC opens or searches the component crates.
- A PC activates the second control in the bubble sequence.
- Combat has not begun by the start of the third round inside the lab.
- A PC picks up the master ledger case.

Four shardlings crawl out of the crates and across the workbenches. They are not immediately focused on killing the PCs. They are attracted to humming crystal, exposed conduits, and the ledger case’s brass fittings because the case was stored beside charged components.

### Shardling Skitterer

**Tiny elemental, unaligned**

**Armor Class:** 13  
**Hit Points:** 7  
**Speed:** 30 ft., climb 20 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 (-2) | 15 (+2) | 12 (+1) | 3 (-4) | 10 (+0) | 5 (-3) |

**Damage Resistances:** Lightning  
**Damage Vulnerabilities:** Thunder  
**Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 10  
**Languages:** —  
**Challenge:** 1/8

#### Actions

**Shard Bite.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 1d6+2 piercing damage.

#### Reactions and traits

**Death Spark.** When the shardling is reduced to 0 hit points, each creature within 5 feet of it must succeed on a DC 11 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d4 lightning damage.

**Unstable Attraction.** The shardling is drawn toward the nearest exposed source of magical energy. If a PC drops a crystal object, casts a spell with a visible energy effect, or activates a pylon control, the nearest shardling may move toward that source instead of attacking the nearest PC. The DM decides; use this to make the creatures feel reactive rather than tactical.

### Encounter tactics

Shardlings should create movement and competing priorities, not a long slugfest.

- One goes for the Energy valve.
- One climbs a workbench and attacks from cover.
- One tries to drag a small crystal tool back toward its crate.
- One approaches whichever PC most recently activated a pylon control.

They do not coordinate like soldiers. They react to sound, light, and vibration.

### Mira’s help during the encounter

Once per round, Mira can do **one** of the following:

- Call out a weak point, granting one PC advantage on their next attack against a shardling.
- Warn a PC, imposing disadvantage on one shardling’s next attack.
- Identify which pylon control is about to pulse, granting advantage on a saving throw against the next pylon arc.
- Tell Jory when to throw his grounding chain.

She cannot do all four. Let the players choose what kind of help they ask for if they think to ask.

### Pylon pulse

If the Calibration Contest ended with two or fewer systems Stable, the pylon acts at **initiative count 20**.

Roll 1d4:

| 1d4 | Effect |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Arc: One random creature within 10 feet of the pylon makes a DC 11 Dexterity save or takes 1d4 lightning damage. |
| 2 | Flash: One random creature looking at the pylon makes a DC 11 Constitution save or is blinded until the start of its next turn. |
| 3 | Shove: Loose tools and unsecured objects slide 5 feet. No creature damage, but one workbench no longer provides cover. |
| 4 | Wrong note: The pylon emits the artificial frequency. Shardlings move 10 feet toward the pylon; Mira gets a free opportunity to identify the pattern for Scene 6. |

If the contest was a clean success, skip the pylon pulse entirely.

### Noncombat solutions

The shardlings can be dealt with without killing all four:

- **Shut down the humming conduit:** Arcana DC 12 or Tinker’s Tools DC 12. The shardlings lose interest and retreat into the crates after one round.
- **Lure them:** Offer a charged shard or humming crystal object and succeed on Persuasion DC 12. Animal Handling is not appropriate unless the PC frames it as rhythm, vibration, and patience; in that case, allow Animal Handling DC 13.
- **Scatter them with thunder:** Any thunder damage causes surviving shardlings to make a DC 11 Wisdom save or flee. They are vulnerable to thunder and dislike the concussion.
- **Trap them:** Close two component crates over them with Athletics or Sleight of Hand DC 12 while another PC distracts them.

### Ending the encounter

End combat when any one of these is true:

- All shardlings are dead, trapped, scattered, or have fled.
- The bubble is down and the remaining shardlings retreat into the vents.
- Twenty-five minutes have passed in the scene and the remaining shardlings can plausibly withdraw.

Do not let cleanup rounds steal time from Mira’s conversation. The conversation is the point of the session.

## If a PC falls to 0 hit points

The shardlings are attracted to the PC’s equipment and dropped objects, not committed to finishing them. On the fallen PC’s next turn, the nearest shardling moves toward a dropped crystal or weapon instead of attacking them.

Mira can throw a grounding rod as her support action, automatically stabilizing the fallen PC if they are within 30 feet and she can see them. She will do this once. Afterward she says:

> “That was my best rod. Your survival is now a financial obligation.”

# Scene 5: The Missing Counterfoil

**Target time:** 25 minutes  
**Status:** Optional if the session is running long

## Trigger

Once the lab is safe, Mira asks for the master ledger.

> “Ledger. Please. I want the shipment record before Den invents a version of events with fewer felonies.”

When she opens the case, she goes still.

One counterfoil has been cut out with a narrow blade. The cuts are clean. The missing page covered the transfer between Coil Yard inspection and Shattermarket storage.

Mira looks up.

> “That page was there when Jory brought the case in.”
>
> “Den. Who has been near the gate?”

Den appears in the doorway, supporting Jory or returning from handing him to a healer.

> “Runner’s gone. Gate crew saw him vault the salvage barrier about thirty seconds after the lab door opened. Flat packet under his coat.”

## If there is no time for the chase

Skip directly to this:

Mira places a sheet of thin wax paper beneath the next ledger page and rubs it with the side of a charcoal stick. The pressure impression of the missing writing appears faintly.

She can recover the shipment facts in Scene 6, but not the Shattermarket storage mark.

She says:

> “Someone wanted the route, not the numbers. Interesting priorities.”

Proceed to Scene 6.

## The chase

**Goal:** Recover the oilskin packet before the runner reaches the salvage gate and disappears into Refraction’s Reach.

The party needs **4 successes before 3 failures**.

Use DC 12 unless an obstacle says otherwise. Each PC may attempt a different action at each obstacle. Count each successful check once; creative spells or tools can grant advantage or replace a check if the use is convincing.

### Obstacle 1: The lab door crowd

Workers surge toward the lab now that the seal is down.

Useful approaches:

- Persuasion: “Medical coming through!”
- Intimidation: Clear the lane by sounding official.
- Acrobatics: Slip through the crowd without knocking anyone into the equipment.

**Failure consequence:** The runner gains distance. Mark one chase failure.

### Obstacle 2: Crane bay

A crane is moving a cracked pylon column across the route.

Useful approaches:

- Perception: Time the load’s swing.
- Athletics: Pull a rolling cart out of the path.
- Acrobatics: Duck beneath the load at the right moment.

**Failure consequence:** A PC must choose between stopping to avoid the load or taking 1d4 bludgeoning damage from a swinging brace and continuing.

### Obstacle 3: Shard pile

The runner crosses a heap of prismglass offcuts. The footing is terrible for anyone not wearing salvage boots.

Useful approaches:

- Survival: Read the stable route.
- Acrobatics: Keep balance across the shifting pieces.
- Athletics: Go around by climbing the sorting frame.

**Failure consequence:** Mark one chase failure and one PC’s speed is reduced by 10 feet for the rest of the chase because of glass in a boot.

### Obstacle 4: Steam vent lane

Timed vents release clouds of scalding steam across the lane.

Useful approaches:

- Investigation: Read the valve timing from the pressure marks.
- Dexterity save: Dash through on a gap.
- Tinker’s Tools: Jam one vent wheel for ten seconds.

**Failure consequence:** One PC takes 1d4 fire damage or marks one chase failure. Let the player choose.

### Obstacle 5: The salvage gate

The runner reaches the gate mechanism and tries to force the small pedestrian hatch.

Useful approaches:

- Athletics: Hold the hatch.
- Sleight of Hand: Catch the packet as he squeezes through.
- Persuasion: Promise Den will go easier on him if he stops.
- Ranged attack: Pin the packet or coat to the gate frame. This is nonlethal unless the player explicitly aims at the runner.

**Failure consequence:** The runner escapes with the packet.

### Obstacle 6: Den’s whistle

Use this only if the PCs are at 3 successes and 2 failures and need one final dramatic beat.

Den finally remembers his signal whistle. The blast makes the runner flinch and look back.

A PC may make one final DC 13 check using any relevant skill they have not already used in the chase.

## If the PCs catch the runner

He drops the packet immediately and puts both hands up.

> “No, no, no. I am paid to carry paper, not be carried by guards.”

If questioned, he reveals:

- A broker’s cutout hired him near the Shattermarket.
- He was told to remove anything stamped or initialled “V.D.”
- He does not know what V.D. stands for.
- He was told not to read the page.
- He was supposed to leave the packet in a rain barrel behind a closed stall.
- Nobody told him anyone would be trapped in the lab.

He is frightened when he learns about Jory. That fear appears genuine on an Insight DC 10.

The PCs can release him, hold him for Den, or try to use him as bait. Do not let this become a second full interrogation scene unless the players are enjoying it and time remains. Mira will cut it short:

> “We have the page. The boy can explain his career choices to Den.”

## If the runner escapes

The packet is gone, but the session does not lose its payoff.

Mira uses the wax-and-charcoal pressure impression method described above. She recovers:

- The shipment authorization.
- The inspection timing.
- The eastern routing notation.

She does **not** recover the Shattermarket storage mark, so the paper trail to Velrix is slightly weaker.

She also concludes that someone knew exactly which page mattered:

> “That was not panic theft. That was editing.”

# Scene 6: Mira’s Price, Paid

**Target time:** 35 minutes  
**Purpose:** Deliver the full ring conversation, let the players interrogate the evidence, and end with a real choice about what to do next.

## Setting the scene

Jory has been taken to a healer. Den is keeping the workers away from the lab. The pylon is dark except for a slow, even pulse.

Mira clears a workbench herself. She does not let Den’s crew help.

She places two objects on a resonance plate:

1. The broken Shardfall ring evidence the PCs brought.
2. The returned ring she tested that day.

Then she sets the master ledger beside them, open to the recovered counterfoil or the pressure impression.

## Read aloud

> Mira strikes a tuning fork and sets it beside the plate.
>
> The Shardfall ring answers first — a thin, uneven note that climbs when it should fall.
>
> The returned ring answers a breath later.
>
> It makes the same wrong climb.
>
> Mira does not look surprised. She looks like a woman who has just watched a bad suspicion become a responsibility.
>
> “Well,” she says. “That is not wear and tear.”

## Mira’s findings

Work through these in order. Let the PCs interrupt, examine the evidence, and ask questions. Mira should show them the proof rather than merely announce conclusions.

### 1. The stamp is genuine

Mira compares the Coil Yard stamp on the Shardfall ring with the die impression in the ledger.

> “That is our stamp. Not copied. Not a Shattermarket imitation. Whoever sent that ring out used our real process.”

**Evidence:** The stamp is slightly offset in the same way as the Yard’s south-bench die, which has a known chipped corner.

### 2. V. Dorne authorized the shipment

The ledger lists the calibration shipment under the authorization initials **V.D.** and the consignment name **Dorne**.

> “V. Dorne authorized the shipment. That is not the same thing as V. Dorne holding the scoring tool, so do not run out of here turning grammar into guilt.”

If the PCs recovered the counterfoil, it also bears a Shattermarket storage mark associated with Dorne’s trade name.

If they did not recover it, Mira has only the ledger’s authorization line and the pressure impression of the routing mark.

### 3. The ring passed inspection before it was altered

This is the session’s new, important refinement.

At inspection, the Coil Yard cuts a hairline **witness mark** across both the ring and its mounting collar. If the ring is later removed or worked on, the mark no longer aligns cleanly.

Mira shows the PCs the scoring on the Shardfall ring:

> “See this hairline? We cut that across ring and collar when we pass a piece. Now look at the scoring.”
>
> She turns the ring under the lamp.
>
> “The scoring cuts through it. That means the scoring was done after my inspectors sealed the piece. This did not leave my Yard like this.”

This does not completely clear everyone who has ever worked at the Coil Yard, and Mira will not claim that it does. It proves the alteration happened after the recorded inspection point: in transit, in storage, or after delivery.

### 4. The scoring is deliberate

The grooves are too regular, too shallow in some places and too precise in others to be impact damage.

> “Storm damage argues with a thing. This persuaded it.”

The scoring changed how the ring seated and how it transferred resonance into the pylon.

### 5. The gain threshold was raised

Mira explains this as plainly as she can:

> “A pylon ring listens for pressure. This one was taught to ignore pressure until there was far too much of it.”
>
> “So instead of bleeding the storm off gradually, the Shardfall pylon held, and held, and then tried to correct everything at once. It broadcast a pulse. The storm answered. From the pylon’s perspective, it was trying to pull the danger closer so it could fix it.”

This confirms sabotage rather than ordinary degradation.

### 6. Shardfall was not the only altered piece

The returned ring Mira tested has the same artificial frequency pattern.

> “One altered ring is a crime. Two from the same shipment is a method.”

Be careful with the scope. Mira does **not** know that all pylons have been altered. She knows at least two components from the same calibration shipment were interfered with.

### 7. A second calibration set is missing

The ledger records another set of components from the same shipment. It was signed out against the eastern maintenance route, but there is no matching receipt confirming that it arrived and was installed correctly.

Mira says:

> “Either the paperwork is incomplete, which I hate, or a whole calibration set went east and nobody can prove where it ended up, which I hate with considerably more energy.”

Do not state with certainty that the Eastern Pylon has already been sabotaged. The correct conclusion is that it must be checked.

### 8. The dwarven notation is mundane

If the PCs ask about the copied dwarven notation from the ring:

Mira identifies it as ordinary workshop notation: tolerance, seating direction, and a warning not to force the ring against the grain of the crystal.

> “It is not a confession. It is not a prophecy. It is a dwarf writing ‘do not be an idiot’ in the traditional format.”

Tordek can confirm this when he returns. It is flavor, not a hidden clue.

## Questions the PCs may ask

### “Did Velrix do it?”

Mira:

> “Dorne’s name authorized the shipment. Dorne’s trade mark appears in the route, if we recovered the right page. That gives you a man to confront and a reason to confront him carefully. It does not put the scoring tool in his hand.”
>
> “If you go in shouting ‘saboteur’ with only this, he will smile, deny it, and know exactly how much you have. I would prefer he learned that later.”

### “Could someone at the Coil Yard have done it?”

Mira does not become defensive now. She is honest.

> “Someone here could have handled it after inspection if they were part of the transport chain. I cannot clear the entire Yard because I am fond of it. But the witness mark tells us where to look: after my bench, before Shardfall.”

### “Why would someone do this?”

Mira:

> “I build stabilization equipment. I can tell you what it does. I cannot tell you what kind of mind looks at a pylon and sees an opportunity instead of a warning.”
>
> “If you want motive, ask the man whose name is on the shipment.”

### “What happened to Rhoric’s team?”

Mira knows only that a team was lost or displaced during the Shardfall incident and that people in the Reach use imprecise language when they are frightened.

> “I can tell you what the ring did. I cannot tell you where the storm put them.”

Do not elaborate.

### “Can you fix the network?”

> “I can fix a pylon I can reach. I cannot fix paperwork that lies to me, and I cannot inspect a ring that has gone east without a receipt.”
>
> “So no. Not from this room.”

### “Will you tell the Reach?”

Mira hesitates. This is the first time she is not brisk.

> “If I announce that someone is altering pylon components, every settlement on the network will demand an inspection tomorrow. We do not have the engineers. Half the crews will start pulling rings that were perfectly safe and install them wrong in a panic.”
>
> “I am not saying keep it secret forever. I am saying choose the moment before someone else chooses it for us.”

## The PCs’ choice

Near the end of the conversation, Mira asks what they intend to do with what they know. There is no mechanically correct answer.

### Option A: Keep it quiet until Tordek returns

**Advantages:**

- Velrix does not learn how much the PCs know.
- The full party can plan together.
- Mira has time to quietly check other local records.

**Cost:**

- The eastern maintenance route remains exposed for another day.

### Option B: Warn the eastern route immediately

**Advantages:**

- Any crew travelling toward the Eastern Pylon can be told to inspect components before installation or activation.
- Mira can send a warning through Yard channels.

**Costs:**

- Word may reach Velrix or his cutouts.
- The warning may be vague enough to cause confusion if it travels too far.

If the PCs choose this, Mira sends Den with a sealed message before the session ends.

### Option C: Confront Velrix as soon as possible

**Advantages:**

- The PCs keep momentum.
- Velrix may react before he knows the evidence has survived.

**Costs:**

- They will be confronting him without Tordek.
- Mira strongly advises against doing it immediately unless they have a plan.

Mira says:

> “If you confront him, do not confront him with everything. Liars relax when they think they know the size of the room they are lying in.”

Record whichever option they choose. It should affect how the next full-party session opens.

## Rewards

### Mira’s trust

Mira now considers the two PCs competent and trustworthy. She gives the party **advantage on the first stabilization-related check** they make during a later pylon encounter.

She also offers free routine repairs at the Coil Yard for damaged mundane equipment damaged during this session.

### Calibration reward

Award according to the Calibration Contest result:

- **Clean success:** Stabilization Shard
- **Partial success:** Damaged Pylon Component and the workers’ respect
- **Rough success:** Prismglass Fragment and a story the Yard workers will exaggerate for months

### Harmonic fork

If the PCs freed Jory, recovered the ledger, and did not recklessly endanger the lab, Mira lends them a **harmonic fork**.

This is a brass case containing a tuning fork and a sliver of responsive crystal.

**Harmonic Fork**  
*Wondrous item, uncommon, session reward*

When the fork is struck in a quiet place, its crystal sliver trembles faintly if a component carrying the specific artificial frequency Mira identified is within 30 feet. The user must spend 1 minute listening and watching the crystal. The fork gives no direction, does not identify the exact object if several crystal objects are nearby, and does not react to ordinary wild magic.

Mira’s warning:

> “It will tell you a wrong note is nearby. It will not tell you who taught it to sing. Do not shake it like a rattle and expect theology.”

If the session went badly, Mira keeps the fork. She still gives the information, but she does not lend Yard equipment to people she does not trust.

### Jory’s gratitude

Before he leaves with the healer, Jory gives one PC his spare brass door wedge. It is mundane, battered, and engraved with his initials.

> “For the next time a door has opinions.”

This is a character beat, not a magic item.

# Running the Session

## Keep Mira present

Mira should not disappear for the first two hours and then appear for a lore dump. Use her constantly:

- Through the window during Scene 1.
- Through the speaking horn during Scene 2.
- Directing, correcting, and worrying during Scene 3.
- Giving orders during Scene 4.
- Arguing with Den during Scene 5.
- Slowing down and becoming thoughtful during Scene 6.

By the time she is free, the players should feel they have been in a conversation with her for the whole session — an interrupted, practical, emergency-shaped conversation.

## Information is never fail-locked

Failure changes the cost, not the destination.

- If the investigation goes badly, Den and Mira push the PCs to the bench.
- If the Calibration Contest goes badly, the door still opens, but the lab is more dangerous.
- If Jory is hurt, Mira is grimmer, but she still talks.
- If the runner escapes, Mira reconstructs the critical facts from the pressure impression.
- If a PC falls, the shardlings go for dropped equipment and Mira stabilizes them once.

The PCs can lose rewards, safety, time, and Mira’s confidence. They should not lose the entire point of the session because of one bad roll.

## If the PCs refuse Mira’s bargain

Let them leave the lab area if they insist. Den will not physically detain them.

After ten minutes of game time, they hear Jory calling for help as another gauge cracks. Mira says, without anger:

> “That is the sound of the conversation becoming shorter.”

If they still refuse, the Yard crew vents the lab. Jory survives with a serious leg injury, the returned ring is destroyed, and Mira gives the PCs only the most basic conclusion: the Shardfall stamp is genuine. The deeper evidence is lost. This should be a real consequence, but do not spend the remaining session punishing them. Move the story forward through the missing eastern set instead.

## If the PCs attack the runner or Yard workers

Den treats this as an emergency, not a duel. Workers use nets, crane hooks, and numbers to separate people rather than trying to kill PCs.

Mira’s trust drops sharply. She still gives the core facts after the emergency if Jory is safe, but she does not lend the harmonic fork and she insists Den remain in the room.

## If the players split up

Allow it during Scene 2. One PC can work the bench while the other talks to Den or watches the window.

During Scene 4, keep both PCs in the lab. The room is small enough that they can pursue different objectives without needing separate maps or long solo sequences.

## If combat runs long

Use these exits:

- After two shardlings die, the survivors retreat if the humming conduit is shut down.
- When the bubble drops, remaining shardlings flee into the vents.
- Jory throws his grounding chain and pins one shardling long enough for the PCs to ignore it.
- Mira opens a maintenance panel and vents steam across the room, forcing shardlings back without damaging the PCs if they are behind cover.

## If the session runs short

Use these expansions:

- Den asks the PCs whether the Yard should vent the lab immediately or trust Mira’s instructions.
- Jory asks one PC whether sabotage means someone wanted the storm to hit Shardfall.
- Mira, through the horn, asks the PCs what Tordek would do with the witness mark.
- After the examination, let the PCs help Mira draft the warning to the eastern route and decide exactly how much it should say.
