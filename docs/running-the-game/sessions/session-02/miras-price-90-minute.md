# Mira’s Price — The 90-Minute Cut

## A complete Coil Yard bottle session for two level 2 PCs

**Runtime:** 90 minutes, hard stops included  
**Location:** The Coil Yard and Mira Flintgear’s harmonics lab, Refraction’s Reach  
**Absent PC:** Tordek  
**Central NPC:** Mira Flintgear, Senior Harmonics Engineer  
**Session goal:** Earn Mira’s analysis of the broken Shardfall pylon ring by helping her contain a live pylon accident and rescue her apprentice.

This is the full script for the short version of *Mira’s Price*. It is not a summary of the longer session — everything you need to run it is on this page. The cuts are structural: the investigation is a montage, the Calibration Contest is four rounds, the lab rescue and the shardling fight happen at the same time, and there is no chase. What survives intact is the spine: Mira trapped and talking from the first minutes, the PCs proving competence, the rescue, and the full ring conversation as the payoff.

---

## The Situation

Earlier today, Mira Flintgear received a returned focusing ring from a maintenance crate. Its serial marks matched the same calibration shipment as the broken ring recovered at Shardfall. When she mounted the returned ring on her laboratory test pylon and brought it up to power, it produced an unnatural, precise frequency — a “wrong note” that kept perfect time.

Mira cleared the lab. Her apprentice, Jory Fen, stayed to record readings and carry in the master shipment ledger. When the test pylon crossed its resonance threshold:

- A containment field snapped around the pylon, trapping Mira beside it.
- The lab’s harmonic door lock engaged.
- A lead-glass blast screen toppled and pinned Jory beneath it.
- Shardlings nesting in charged component crates began to stir.

The two PCs arrive minutes later, carrying the Shardfall ring evidence. Mira can see them through the lab’s crystal window and speak through a brass speaking horn. She will examine their ring — after they help her get Jory out and keep her pylon from venting into the Yard.

### The pylons are small

Mira’s test pylon is a human-scale machine, about **10 feet tall**: a crystal column on a rotating brass base, its three focusing rings at chest, head, and just-above-head height. A technician services it from the floor or a step platform. It is dangerous because of the energy it channels, not because of its size.

## Running Clock

There is no break in this version. Hold the hard stops and the session fits.

| Clock | Scene | Hard stop |
|---:|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | Scene 1: The Sealed Lab | Mira’s bargain is made |
| 0:07–0:16 | Scene 2: Reading the Yard | At 0:16, Den sends them to the bench |
| 0:16–0:34 | Scene 3: The Calibration Contest | At the end of round 4, the door opens |
| 0:34–0:58 | Scene 4: Inside the Lab | When the bubble drops, the scene ends |
| 0:58–1:01 | Scene 5: The Missing Page | No chase; Mira uses the pressure impression |
| 1:01–1:25 | Scene 6: Mira’s Price, Paid | At 1:25, Mira stops taking questions |
| 1:25–1:30 | Scene 7: The Decision | Choice made, closing line, done |

If you fall behind anywhere, steal time from Scene 6 questions — never from the five facts in the Scene 6 checklist.

### What the PCs must learn

- The Coil Yard stamp on the Shardfall ring is genuine.
- The ring belonged to a calibration shipment authorized under the name **V. Dorne**.
- The ring passed Coil Yard inspection, and its witness mark proves it was scored **after** inspection — in transit or storage.
- The scoring is deliberate and raised the ring’s gain threshold, so the Shardfall pylon broadcast a harmonic pulse and pulled the storm toward itself.
- The returned ring Mira tested today carries the same artificial frequency. Shardfall was not a one-off.
- A second calibration set from the shipment is unaccounted for, logged against the eastern maintenance route.
- The evidence points toward Velrix Dorne but does **not** prove he personally altered the ring, and it reveals nothing about his motive.

### What this session must not reveal

- Velrix’s motive.
- Anything about resonance catalysts or stress-testing the pylon network.
- The fate of Rhoric’s team.
- Anything about the Thirteenth Hour or the Sixth Fallen Dawn.
- Any connection between Iri Vale and anyone else named Vale.

## Preparation

You need:

- A tracker for three systems: Harmonic Alignment, Structural Pressure, Energy Flow — each Stable, Strained, Critical, or Overload.
- A rough sketch of the lab (Scene 4), three shardling tokens, and tokens for Mira’s correction and Den’s crew brace.
- The Scene 6 checklist and Mira’s note, printed or open, for the payoff.

### Tordek’s absence

One sentence, then move on:

> Tordek has remained behind to watch the party’s other evidence and recover from the night beneath the clock tower. The two of you have brought the ring evidence to the Coil Yard yourselves.

If Tordek is physically holding the original ring at your table, the PCs instead bring a detached section of the broken ring, a full-size rubbing of the stamp and scoring, and their Shardfall measurements. Mira can work from any of those.

### The NPCs

**Mira Flintgear** — rock gnome, Senior Harmonics Engineer. Ink-smudged fingers, soot-flecked goggles, tools arranged with obsessive precision even mid-emergency. Fast-talking and exact; she respects competence instantly and has no patience for vague reassurance.

> “That is not a hum. A hum has manners. That is a scream learning pitch.”
>
> “Good. Competent. I retract two of the things I was thinking about you.”

**Jory Fen** — her apprentice. Young, gangly, brave, pinned under the blast screen with one boot trapped. He jokes when he is frightened.

> “I’m fine. This is a load-bearing screen. I’m providing structural support.”
>
> “If Mira asks, I absolutely left when she told me to. Twice.”

**Dennet “Den” Rusk** — shift foreman. Broad, forearms covered in old burns, protective of his crew and terrified the Yard will be blamed for Shardfall. He is obstructive for the first two minutes and an ally after that. He is not corrupt and knows nothing about the sabotage.

**The runner** — an unnamed salvage courier hired through a cutout to steal one page from the master ledger. In this version he is already gone before he can be chased. He matters as evidence that someone is actively tidying up, not as a scene.

# Scene 1: The Sealed Lab

**0:00–0:07**

## Read aloud

> Heat rolls across the Coil Yard in waves of steam and ozone. Workers are moving quickly, but nobody is shouting. That is somehow worse.
>
> A brass alarm bell hangs silent above the yard. Beneath it, every worker keeps glancing toward a squat reinforced building marked with black-and-yellow harmonic warnings.
>
> Its crystal window is fogged from the inside. Its door is shut. Red warning crystals pulse above the frame.
>
> Through the glass, a shape moves. A small hand wipes a clear circle in the condensation.
>
> Mira Flintgear’s face appears. She raises her goggles, looks at the two of you, and mouths one word:
>
> “Finally.”

Den steps between the PCs and the door. He does not draw a weapon; he plants himself like a man used to stopping crane loads with hand signals.

> “No. Absolutely not. That lab is under harmonic seal, the pylon inside is live, and I have two people trapped behind that door. Unless you are here to help, you can be somewhere else very quickly.”

The moment the PCs mention Mira, the ring, or the Coil Yard stamp, Mira raps on the window. Her voice comes through the speaking horn beside the door, flattened and loud.

> “Den. Let them stay where I can see them.”
>
> “Mira—”
>
> “I did not say open the door. I said let them stay.”

She looks at the evidence case.

> “That’s the Shardfall ring? Or enough of it. Before you begin, understand my position. My apprentice is pinned under a blast screen in a room with an unhappy pylon. My lab is sealed. My yard is one bad pulse from becoming a crater with excellent drainage.”
>
> “You want to ask me about a Coil Yard stamp. I want my people alive and my lab intact. Help me achieve my list, and I will give your list my complete attention.”

## If they agree

Mira softens by exactly one degree:

> “Good. Competence may yet save us all paperwork. Den will show you the bench. Jory is inside with me — alive, conscious, and making jokes, which means his leg hurts more than he is admitting. The pylon is behind a containment bubble with me. Do not ask me who stamped what until those facts have changed.”

## If they try to force the door

The door is a harmonic lock, not a barred door. A Strength check DC 15 makes it shudder but not open, and anyone touching the frame makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 lightning damage. Mira, through the horn:

> “Do not pry at a containment lock while it is deciding whether to be a door or a weapon!”

Den points at the external control bench:

> “The lock answers to the lab feed. You want in, you stabilize the feed. That’s what the bench is for.”

## What Mira will and won’t discuss now

She answers questions about whether she and Jory are alive, what opens the lab, and immediate safety. She will not discuss the stamp, the shipment, or V. Dorne yet:

> “I am not refusing. I am sequencing. Dying would make me considerably less informative.”

At **0:07**, move to Scene 2 regardless of where the conversation has reached.

# Scene 2: Reading the Yard

**0:07–0:16**

A nine-minute montage. Each PC picks **one** route and makes **one** check. If both PCs pick the same route, the second grants the first advantage. Every route gives its basic information even on a failure; success earns the bonus.

Den frames it:

> “You’ve got until that gauge crosses the red. Look sharp, ask fast, then work the bench.”

## Route A: Mira’s workbench

Beside the control bench, under a canvas awning: a harmonic trace slate still drawing a jagged line, a cup of tea with a skin on it, three tuning forks in a precise row — and a fourth space, empty. Mira’s note, in small furious handwriting:

> **Not wear — scored. Same wrong note. Do not let Den vent the lab.**

**Check:** Investigation or Arcana DC 12.

- **Basic information:** The trace shows the lab pylon’s frequency. One artificial spike repeats at exact intervals. Storm interference is irregular; this is not.
- **Bonus on a success:** The PC recognizes the containment cycle. **Advantage on the first Harmonic Alignment check** in Scene 3.

## Route B: Den and the workers

Den is watching a pressure gauge whose needle creeps toward a red band. He talks while he works.

**Check:** Persuasion DC 11 — or no check at all if the PC asks about Jory before asking about the ring or the stamp. Den respects that order of priorities.

- **Basic information, given freely:** Mira received a returned ring this morning, mounted it on the lab pylon at low power, and ordered the lab cleared almost immediately. Jory followed her in with the master ledger. The door sealed less than a minute later.
- **Bonus on a success (or the Jory-first approach):** Den grants the **crew brace** for Scene 3, and adds what Mira said before she went in:

> “She argued with the courier first. Said the ring was singing in the wrong key. I told her returned stock sings all kinds of wrong after a storm. She told me storms don’t keep time.”
>
> “Then she took the ledger and said, ‘If this is what I think it is, nobody touches the paperwork until I’m done.’”

## Route C: Through the window

Jory is visible when the condensation shifts, lying beneath the edge of the blast screen. He can mouth words and use hand signals.

**Check:** Perception or Investigation DC 10.

- **Basic information:** Jory is awake and tapping the floor in a steady rhythm. He is alive and trying to communicate.
- **Bonus on a success:** He walks them through the containment sequence with hand signals — one finger, point at the Alignment dial; two fingers, point at the conduit cabinet, mime flowing; three fingers, point at the Pressure release:

> **Alignment first. Energy second. Pressure last.**

## If nobody picked Route C

At **0:15**, just before Den calls time, Jory raps hard on the inside of the window and performs the same signals unprompted. The sequence is never locked behind one route choice.

## Hard stop

At **0:16** the pressure gauge beside Den cracks with a sharp *snap*. Mira, through the horn:

> “That was the inexpensive warning. The next one will have opinions. Bench. Now.”

Begin Scene 3.

# Scene 3: The Calibration Contest

**0:16–0:34 — four rounds maximum**

The PCs stabilize the lab’s external power feed at the control bench. The three systems — **Harmonic Alignment**, **Structural Pressure**, **Energy Flow** — all begin **Strained**.

## Read aloud

> The control bench is a slab of brass, crystal, and scarred wood built by someone who expected it to be used during emergencies.
>
> A tuning dial sings faintly under its glass cover. A pressure wheel ticks, pauses, ticks again, each movement a fraction wider than the last. A row of crystal conduits brightens and dims in uneven sequence.
>
> Through the lab window, Mira presses one palm to the glass.
>
> “Four cycles before Den has to vent the room. I would prefer not to be vented. Begin.”

## Round structure

Each round:

1. Roll 1d6 — one system worsens one step (Stable → Strained → Critical → Overload).
2. Each PC takes one action on one system.
3. Mira comments. That’s the round.

| 1d6 | System that worsens |
|---:|---|
| 1–2 | Harmonic Alignment |
| 3–4 | Structural Pressure |
| 5–6 | Energy Flow |

## Actions

**Harmonic Alignment — DC 12.** Arcana, Performance, or Insight: hum the pulse, tap the crystal in rhythm, match Mira’s hand movements through the window. *Failure:* the next Alignment check is at disadvantage.

**Structural Pressure — DC 11.** Athletics, Smith’s Tools, or Tinker’s Tools: turn the pressure wheel against resistance, brace the frame, wedge a support into place. *Failure:* a steam burst pushes the PC back 5 feet or deals 1 damage — player’s choice.

**Energy Flow — DC 12.** Arcana, Sleight of Hand, or Investigation: reroute conduits, ground excess charge, follow the sequence of lights. *Failure:* an arc deals 1 lightning damage to the acting PC or the nearest ally.

A success improves the system one step.

**Working together:** If both PCs work the same system in the same round and both succeed, it improves **two steps**. One success improves it one step. If both fail, apply only one failure consequence.

## The two one-time helps

**Mira’s Flintgear correction (once).** The PCs may spend it to reroll one failed check, or to learn which system will worsen next round.

> “No — half a turn back. There. Do you hear it? Neither do I. That is the point. Hold it.”

**Den’s crew brace (once, only if earned in Scene 2).** When a system would worsen, the PCs may spend the brace to prevent it.

> “Rusk crew! Hands on and pretend you like these people!”

## Overload

If a system reaches Overload: every PC at the bench makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 lightning or force damage; the system resets to Critical; and if Mira’s correction is still unused, it is lost — she is busy keeping the pylon from venting. Overload is dangerous and embarrassing. It does not end the session.

## Escalation beats

- **Round 1:** Tools on nearby benches vibrate in time with the lab pylon.
- **Round 2:** The fog inside the window brightens with each pulse.
- **Round 3:** Jory stops joking. Mira glances toward him more often.
- **Round 4:** Den puts his hand on the manual vent control. He does not want to use it.

## Ending the contest

If all three systems are Stable at the end of any round, the contest ends early — clean. Otherwise, at the end of round 4, Den forces the manual release and the door opens anyway. Count the Stable systems:

**Clean — all three Stable.** The door opens with a soft chime. The pylon’s feed is calm; no pulses inside the lab. *Reward: Stabilization Shard.*

> Mira: “All three. At once. Den, witness that. I may need someone to confirm I said something nice.”

**Partial — two Stable.** The door opens. The pylon stays live: one pulse inside the lab (Scene 4). *Reward: Damaged Pylon Component.*

> Mira: “Good enough to enter. Not good enough to relax. Those are different categories.”

**Rough — zero or one Stable.** Den forces the release during a venting pulse: each PC makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 force damage as the door blasts open. The pylon pulses every round inside the lab, and the containment bubble shoves on its first failed shutdown attempt. *Reward: Prismglass Fragment.*

> Mira: “You opened it. I am choosing gratitude over precision.”

# Scene 4: Inside the Lab

**0:34–0:58**

The rescue and the fight are one scene. The PCs split their attention between Jory, the bubble sequence, and the shardlings — that tension is the encounter.

## The room

About 30 by 25 feet. Sketch it in a minute:

- **South:** the door the PCs enter through.
- **Center:** the 10-foot test pylon on its rotating base.
- **North of the pylon:** Mira, inside the containment bubble.
- **East workbench:** Jory under the fallen blast screen; the master ledger case is trapped under the screen’s lower edge beside his boot.
- **West wall:** conduit cabinets, grounding chains, the Energy valve.
- **South-east corner:** open component crates — the shardlings’ nest.
- Workbenches give half cover to anyone who says they’re behind one.

## Read aloud

> The lab smells of hot brass, ozone, and tea that has boiled dry.
>
> The test pylon stands in the center of the room — ten feet of crystal, brass, and bad temper, its three rings turning at slightly different speeds. Light moves up the column in pulses.
>
> Mira is on the far side, separated from you by a curved field of translucent energy. She looks furious, relieved, and determined to admit to neither.
>
> To your right, a lead-glass blast screen has fallen across the workbench. Beneath it, Jory Fen raises one hand.
>
> “Still providing structural support,” he says. “Any time you’re ready.”

Mira points in sequence — at Jory, at the three controls around the pylon base, at herself:

> “Him first if you can manage it. Then the bubble. Alignment, Energy, Pressure. Do not improvise the order because you feel creatively oppressed.”

## Objective 1: Free Jory — one check

The screen’s frame has caught on the workbench and one of Jory’s boots is trapped under a support strut. **One successful check** frees him:

- **Athletics DC 12** — lift or lever the screen while he crawls.
- **Tinker’s Tools DC 12** — work the scaffold jack under the frame.
- **Sleight of Hand DC 11** — release the boot clasp without shifting the screen.
- **Medicine DC 10** — while the other PC braces the screen (Help), guide him out without wrenching the leg.

On a failure, the screen settles with a grinding shriek and Jory winces — but he has wriggled halfway free, so **the next rescue attempt has advantage**. Do not let this stall.

Once free, Jory limps to the door dragging the **ledger case** out from under the screen with him. No separate check: the ledger is recovered. He can also hold the Energy valve open on Mira’s instruction, granting advantage on the Energy step (below), and then he gets out of the room.

If the bubble drops before Jory is freed, Mira crosses the room and helps: the rescue resolves in narration as the scene ends.

## Objective 2: Drop the bubble — the sequence

Three controls around the pylon base, activated **in order**, each an action and a check:

1. **Alignment dial** — Arcana or Performance DC 12.
2. **Energy valve** — Arcana, Sleight of Hand, or Tinker’s Tools DC 12.
3. **Pressure release** — Athletics or Tinker’s Tools DC 11.

**Mira’s guidance:** once per round, Mira can give one PC **advantage** on a sequence check — she is watching the gauges from inside and calling the moment.

**Out of order:** the bubble flashes; the activating PC makes a Dexterity save DC 11 or takes 1d4 force damage; the sequence resets and the correct control pulses more brightly. On a **rough** contest result, the first failed attempt also pushes everyone within 10 feet of the pylon back 5 feet.

## The shardlings — three, arriving mid-sequence

Three shardlings crawl out of the component crates when the **first** sequence control is activated (or the moment a PC searches the crates). They are drawn to humming crystal and exposed conduits, not bent on killing anyone.

### Shardling Skitterer

**Tiny elemental, unaligned**  
**AC** 13 · **HP** 7 · **Speed** 30 ft., climb 20 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 (−2) | 15 (+2) | 12 (+1) | 3 (−4) | 10 (+0) | 5 (−3) |

**Resistances:** lightning · **Vulnerabilities:** thunder  
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 10

**Shard Bite.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 1d6+2 piercing.

**Death Spark.** When reduced to 0 HP, each creature within 5 feet makes a DC 11 Dexterity save or takes 1d4 lightning damage.

**Unstable Attraction.** A shardling moves toward the nearest exposed energy source — a PC who just activated a control, a dropped crystal object, the valve a PC is holding. Use this instead of optimal tactics; they should feel reactive, not coordinated.

### Running them fast

- One goes for the Energy valve. One climbs a workbench and bites from cover. One goes for whoever activated the last control.
- **Noncombat exits:** shut down the humming conduit (Arcana or Tinker’s Tools DC 12) and they retreat within a round; lure them with a humming crystal (Persuasion DC 12); thunder damage makes each survivor save Wisdom DC 11 or flee.
- **If a PC drops to 0 HP:** the shardlings go for their dropped equipment, not the body. One stabilization is free — Mira’s grounding rod if she is free, a rod Den tosses through the door if she isn’t. Do not spend table time on death saves tonight.
- **The scene ends when the bubble drops.** Surviving shardlings scatter into the vents the instant the field collapses. No cleanup rounds.

## The pylon pulse

Depends on the Scene 3 result:

- **Clean:** no pulse.
- **Partial:** once only — at initiative count 20 in round 2, one random creature within 10 feet of the pylon makes a DC 11 Dexterity save or takes 1d4 lightning damage.
- **Rough:** the same pulse at initiative count 20 **every round**.

## When the bubble drops — read aloud

> The third control settles into place.
>
> For one heartbeat, every light in the lab goes out.
>
> Then the containment field collapses inward with a sound like a struck wineglass, and Mira Flintgear steps through the fading rings of light with the expression of a woman preparing several complaints in alphabetical order.

Mira’s first words, while she checks Jory and the pylon:

> “Ledger. Please. I want the shipment record before Den invents a version of events with fewer felonies.”

Move straight to Scene 5. If the clock is past **0:58**, trim Scene 5 to Mira’s first two lines and the impression result, and start Scene 6 by **1:01** no matter what.

# Scene 5: The Missing Page

**0:58–1:01 — three minutes, no chase**

Jory sets the ledger case on the workbench — or Den does, if Jory has already been taken to the healer. Mira opens it, runs one finger down the counterfoils — and stops.

## Read aloud

> One page has been cut out with a narrow blade. The cuts are clean. The missing page covered the transfer between Coil Yard inspection and Shattermarket storage.
>
> Mira looks up. “That page was there when Jory brought the case in.”

Den, from the doorway:

> “Then your runner took it. Gate crew saw him vault the salvage barrier about thirty seconds after the lab door opened. Flat packet under his coat. He’s gone, Mira.”

> “Of course he is.”

Mira doesn’t chase paper. She lays thin wax paper beneath the next page and rubs it with the side of a charcoal stick. The pressure of the missing writing rises, faint and readable:

- The shipment authorization — **V.D.**
- The eastern routing notation.
- **Not** the Shattermarket storage mark. That was on the stolen page, and it is gone.

> “Someone wanted the route, not the numbers. And they knew exactly which page mattered. That was not panic theft. That was editing.”

What the PCs know now: somebody connected to this shipment has a cutout watching the Coil Yard, and they moved the moment the lab opened. What it costs them: the paper trail to Velrix is the authorization line only — a name to investigate, not a storage record. Mira proceeds to the ring examination with what she has.

# Scene 6: Mira’s Price, Paid

**1:01–1:25 — protect this scene; it is the point of the session**

Jory has been taken to a healer. Den is keeping the workers away. The pylon is dark except for one slow, even pulse. Mira clears a workbench herself and lays out three things: the Shardfall ring evidence, the returned ring she tested today, and the master ledger.

## Read aloud

> Mira strikes a tuning fork and sets it beside the plate.
>
> The Shardfall ring answers first — a thin, uneven note that climbs when it should fall.
>
> The returned ring answers a breath later.
>
> It makes the same wrong climb.
>
> Mira does not look surprised. She looks like a woman who has just watched a bad suspicion become a responsibility.
>
> “Well,” she says. “That is not wear and tear.”

Work through the five findings in order. Let the players interrupt and handle the evidence; Mira shows the proof rather than announcing conclusions. If the clock reaches **1:25**, stop taking new questions and go to Scene 7.

### 1. The stamp is genuine, and V. Dorne authorized the shipment

Mira compares the stamp on the Shardfall ring with the die impression in the ledger — same chipped corner, same slight offset as the Yard’s south-bench die.

> “That is our stamp. Not copied. Not a Shattermarket imitation. Whoever sent that ring out used our real process.”
>
> “And the ledger is plain: the calibration shipment went out under the authorization of **V. Dorne**.”

### 2. The ring was altered after inspection — the witness mark

At inspection, the Coil Yard cuts a hairline **witness mark** across both a ring and its mounting collar. Mira turns the Shardfall ring under the lamp so the scoring catches the light.

> “See this hairline? We cut that across ring and collar when we pass a piece. Now look at the scoring.”
>
> “The scoring cuts *through* it. That means the scoring was done after my inspectors sealed the piece. This did not leave my Yard like this.”
>
> “After my bench. Before Shardfall. That is your window: transit, or storage.”

### 3. The scoring raised the gain threshold

> “A pylon ring listens for pressure. This one was taught to ignore pressure until there was far too much of it.”
>
> “So instead of bleeding the storm off gradually, the Shardfall pylon held, and held, and then tried to correct everything at once. It broadcast a pulse. The storm answered. From the pylon’s perspective, it was trying to pull the danger closer so it could fix it.”

The grooves are too regular and precise to be impact damage:

> “Storm damage argues with a thing. This persuaded it.”

### 4. Shardfall was not the only altered piece

She taps the returned ring — the one that trapped her in her own lab today.

> “One altered ring is a crime. Two from the same shipment is a method.”
>
> “I do not know how far the method reaches. Do not let anyone tell you I said every pylon on the network is compromised. I said *this shipment*.”

### 5. A second calibration set is missing, east

The ledger records another set of components from the same shipment, signed out against the **eastern maintenance route**, with no arrival receipt and no installation mark.

> “Either the paperwork is incomplete, which I hate, or a whole calibration set went east and nobody can prove where it ended up, which I hate with considerably more energy.”

Do not let this harden into certainty that the Eastern Pylon is already sabotaged. The correct conclusion is that it has to be checked.

## Questions they will ask

**“Did Velrix do it?”**

> “Dorne’s name bought the shipment — that is what the paper says. It does not put the scoring tool in his hand, and the page that might have tied his storage to it is currently under a runner’s coat.”
>
> “You have a man to confront, and a reason to confront him carefully. If you go in shouting ‘saboteur’ with only this, he will smile, deny it, and know exactly how much you have. I would prefer he learned that later.”

**“Could someone at the Coil Yard have done it?”**

Mira is honest, not defensive:

> “Someone here could have handled it after inspection if they were in the transport chain. I cannot clear the entire Yard because I am fond of it. But the witness mark tells you where to look.”

**“Why would someone do this?”**

> “I build stabilization equipment. I can tell you what it does. I cannot tell you what kind of mind looks at a pylon and sees an opportunity instead of a warning. If you want motive, ask the man whose name is on the shipment.”

**“What happened to Rhoric’s team?”**

> “I can tell you what the ring did. I cannot tell you where the storm put them.”

Nothing further. Change the subject back to the evidence.

**“What about the dwarven writing on the ring?”**

Ordinary workshop notation — tolerance, seating direction, and a warning not to force the ring against the grain of the crystal.

> “It is not a confession. It is not a prophecy. It is a dwarf writing ‘do not be an idiot’ in the traditional format. Tordek will confirm it, and enjoy doing so.”

## Rewards — one minute, no negotiation scene

- **Calibration reward** from Scene 3: Stabilization Shard (clean), Damaged Pylon Component (partial), or Prismglass Fragment (rough).
- **Mira’s trust:** the party has **advantage on the first stabilization-related check** at a later pylon encounter, and routine repairs at the Coil Yard are free for gear damaged tonight.
- **Harmonic Fork** — Mira lends it if Jory was freed and the lab wasn’t recklessly endangered: a brass case holding a tuning fork and a sliver of responsive crystal. Struck in a quiet place and given a minute, the crystal trembles if a component carrying the artificial frequency is within 30 feet. No direction, no exact object, no reaction to ordinary wild magic.

> “It will tell you a wrong note is nearby. It will not tell you who taught it to sing. Do not shake it like a rattle and expect theology.”

If the session went badly, Mira keeps the fork but still gives every fact above.

# Scene 7: The Decision

**1:25–1:30**

Mira closes the ledger but doesn’t put it away. One question, two options, take the first answer the table gives you.

> “Last thing, and then I am done being informative for one day.”
>
> “I can send a warning east tonight — sealed, Yard channels, tells the route crews to check every component before it is installed or powered. It may save somebody. It may also tell whoever did this that the evidence survived.”
>
> “Or we sit on it until Tordek is back and your whole party decides together. The eastern route stays exposed one more day, and Dorne learns nothing.”
>
> “Which risk do you want?”

**If they warn the east:** Den leaves with Mira’s sealed message before the session ends. Mira’s warning says “inspect before powering” — nothing about sabotage. Note the choice; word may reach Velrix’s cutouts before the full party acts.

**If they keep it quiet:** Mira locks the ledger in her office safe herself. Note the choice; the eastern route remains exposed, and Velrix learns nothing yet.

**If they can’t decide by 1:29:** Mira decides for the night:

> “Then it waits until morning. Indecision is also a decision; at least this one is mine.”

# If Things Go Sideways

**They refuse the bargain.** Let them walk. Ten minutes later they hear Jory calling for help as another gauge cracks, and Mira, without anger: “That is the sound of the conversation becoming shorter.” If they still refuse, the crew vents the lab; Jory survives with a serious leg injury, the returned ring is destroyed, and Mira gives only the basic conclusion — the stamp is genuine. End on the missing eastern set.

**They attack the workers or Den.** Den treats it as an emergency, not a duel: nets, crane hooks, numbers. Mira still gives the core facts if Jory is safe, but no harmonic fork, and Den stays in the room for Scene 6.

**They split up.** Fine in Scene 2 — that’s the design. In Scene 4, keep both PCs in the lab; the room is small enough for separate objectives on one map.

**A scene runs long.** The hard stops are the fix, in order: Den calls time (0:16), Den forces the door (end of round 4), the bubble dropping ends Scene 4, Mira stops taking questions (1:25). Use them in character and without apology.
