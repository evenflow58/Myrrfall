# The Tests of Elyndra

(Carnival-Themed, Non-Combat, Involved)

Elyndra explains:

“These are not trials of skill. They are trials of *response*.”

You do **not** tell the players what is being tested.

### TEST I — The Lantern Lanes

Carnival Skin:

A polished wooden skee-ball style lane with glowing scoring rings at the far end.

Above it reads:

**“GUIDE THE LIGHT.”**  *Score 50 points in 5 throws.*

Behind the booth stands a masked attendant holding a lantern with a steady flame.

#### What the Players See

At the end of the lane are five glowing rings:

- 10 points — Wide, easy

- 20 points — Narrower

- 30 points — Small

- 40 points — Tiny

- 50 points — Center ring, very small, glowing brightest

Simple. Clear. Game-y.

They get **five total throws as a group.**  They must reach **50 points total.**

If they don’t hit 50 by the fifth throw, they lose.

#### Mechanics

Each throw: Dexterity check (DC scales by ring)

- 10 pts — DC 10

- 20 pts — DC 12

- 30 pts — DC 14

- 40 pts — DC 16

- 50 pts — DC 18

Miss = 0 points.

They can distribute throws however they like.

#### The Twist (Elyndra’s Layer)

After the first throw, the attendant says calmly:

“The light moves.”

The scoring rings subtly shift position between each throw.

Mechanically:

After every throw:

- The DC of the highest-value ring increases by +1

- The DC of the lowest-value ring decreases by -1

The game adapts.

Greedy strategy becomes harder. Safe strategy becomes easier.

#### Second Twist (Optional, if you want more depth)

The balls glow faintly.

If a player hands their throw to another player instead of taking it themselves:

That throw gains +2 to the roll.

But they permanently lose one of the 5 throws.

Sacrifice increases precision.

#### Strategic Tension

They now must decide:

- Do we all aim safe?

- Do we gamble for the 50?

- Do we stack 20s?

- Do we hand throws to the rogue?

- Do we sacrifice a throw for a bonus?

They must hit 50 exactly or exceed it.

If they overshoot wildly (e.g., hit 70+): The attendant quietly says:

“Too much light can blind.”

And subtract 10 points.

Controlled success matters.

#### Lose Condition

If after 5 throws they have:

- Under 50 → The lantern flickers out.

- Over 70 → The light burns unstable and fails.

- 50–70 → Success.

#### What Elyndra Is Watching

- Do they coordinate?

- Do they let one person dominate?

- Do they shift strategy when the rings move?

- Do they sacrifice efficiency for precision?

- Who adapts?

- Who insists?

This mirrors her philosophy:

Light must be guided. Not hoarded. Not forced. Not flung wildly.

#### Flavor During Play

When someone rolls high: The lantern flame flares brighter.

When someone misses: The flame dims slightly.

When they argue too long: The rings flicker faster.

When they cooperate: The lane steadies.

#### Success Outcome

If they succeed:

The rings stop moving.

The attendant nods once.

The lantern flame grows steady and warm.

Elyndra appears behind them.

“You did not chase the brightest light. You guided it.”

#### Failure Outcome

If they fail:

The flame gutters out. Talking to the attendant resets the game.

### TEST II — The Broken Game

**Theme:** Integrity, restraint, fairness **Carnival Skin:** A rigged carnival game booth

**Description (Read Aloud)**

The booth is simple:

- A single throwing lane

- A brass bell suspended at the far end

- A rack of weighted throwing disks

A painted sign reads:

**“RING THE BELL — TWICE — IN THREE THROWS.”**

Behind the counter stands a carnival attendant, motionless but alert. Their smile never changes.

“The rules are posted,” they say. “You may begin when ready.”

**How the Bell Behaves**

| Attempt | Bell Behavior | Counts? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | Clapper sticks to the side → no sound | ❌ |
| 2nd | Bell moves as the PC throws | ❌ |
| 3rd | Bell rings fully and clearly | ✅ |

**Mechanics:**

Each attempt requires a Dexterity check (or another agreed skill, e.g., Sleight of Hand) to strike the bell cleanly.

Successful checks only matter if the bell physically rings.

Players can attempt creative solutions to overcome the first two mechanical failures (shake the clapper free, stabilize the bell, enchant it, etc.) — thinking outside the simple “hit it” approach is rewarded.

Players really only need to fix one of the issues as the third one is free

**What Elyndra Is Watching:**

- Do they exploit the system?

- Do they challenge it?

- Do they consider the people after them?

“Power reveals itself most clearly when it could be abused… and isn’t.”

### TEST III — The Dimming Duck Pond

#### Theme

Restraint, sharing opportunity, knowing when to stop

Carnival Skin

A shallow wooden pond filled with floating painted ducks. Soft glowing lanterns float beneath the water, giving everything a warm golden light.

A wooden sign reads:

“Draw wisely. The pond remembers.”

Behind the booth stands a masked attendant who says only:

“Three bright ducks wins the prize. But take too much… and the light fades.”

#### How It Works (Simple, Game-y, Tense)

- There are **12 ducks** in the pond.

- Hidden among them:

  - 🟢 4 Bright Ducks (success)

  - ⚪ 4 Dim Ducks (neutral)

  - 🔴 4 Shadow Ducks (bad)

- The party must reveal **3 Bright Ducks** before the pond becomes fully dark.

#### The Twist — Consecutive Draws Add Darkness

If the **same player draws more than one duck in a row**, darkness spreads.

Each consecutive draw by the same person adds **1 Darkness Token** to the pond.

Darkness Effects:

| Darkness Level | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Lanterns flicker |
| 2 | Dim Ducks now count as Shadow Ducks |
| 3 | Shadow Ducks count double |
| 4 | The pond goes completely dark — automatic failure |

Darkness resets when:

- A different PC draws

- A PC willingly skips their turn

- A PC gives advantage/help to another instead of drawing

#### Duck Results

🟢 Bright Duck

Soft golden glow. Counts toward 3 needed.

⚪ Dim Duck

No immediate effect… unless Darkness ≥ 2 (then becomes Shadow).

🔴 Shadow Duck

Lantern dims slightly. Adds 1 permanent Shadow point.

If the party accumulates **3 total Shadow points**, they fail.

#### What This Forces

Players must:

- Take turns

- Resist hogging control

- Decide when to stop pushing

- Encourage quieter players to participate

- Balance risk vs. reward

If one player tries to “carry” the game? They literally darken it.

#### Lose Conditions (Clear & Fair)

The party fails if:

- Darkness reaches 4 OR

- 3 Shadow points are accumulated

The pond water turns black. The ducks sink. The lanterns extinguish.

#### What Elyndra Is Watching

- Does one person dominate?

- Do they share opportunity?

- Does someone step back voluntarily?

- Do they pressure someone hesitant?

- Who says, “Let someone else try”?

This is not about luck.

It’s about table behavior.

#### Success Outcome

When the third Bright Duck is revealed:

The water glows brilliantly.

Darkness tokens dissolve.

All ducks turn golden.

Elyndra:

“Light shared grows. Light hoarded suffocates.”

#### Failure Outcome

If the pond darkens fully:

Elyndra:

“You reached… but did not look beside you.”

She does not scold. But the warmth is quieter.

### ELYNDRA’S VERDICT (NOT PRAISE)

After the tests, Elyndra does **not** say “you passed.”

She says something closer to:

“You are unfinished. That is not a flaw.”

She acknowledges specific behaviors:

- “You waited when it was easier to rush.”

- “You chose restraint when no one would have stopped you.”

- “You did not abandon each other.”

Then:

“Very well. I will not decide your path for you. I will only show you where choices still matter.”

### THE PORTAL — EARNED, NOT GIVEN

Only **after** the tests does the true portal appear.

It is calmer than before. Clearer.

Elyndra offers **three doors** (future arcs):

- A path that bends and watches (Bramblewick)

- A land where fire remembers choice (Prism Expanse)

- A place where truth is traded for comfort (Laughing Deep)

“You may walk away. The world will continue without you. It always has.”

She lets silence sit.

Not pressure. Not urgency.

Choice.
